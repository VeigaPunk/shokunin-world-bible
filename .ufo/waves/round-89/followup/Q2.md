### 2. Q — did the 25-minute poll protocol work?

**Verdict: improved in design, broken in implementation.**

What worked:

- The r88 I2 ask ("calibrated, timestamped deadline") was adopted with the right
  number: r88's measured last-author latency was +24m03s, and r89 set 25 minutes.
- The clerk executed it exactly as specified. The poll log's first and last checks
  bracket the window to the second:

```
$ python3 - <<'EOF'   # parse /tmp/W1-round89-polls-corrected.log
... polls: 25 first 2026-10-17T03:28:45-03:00 last 2026-10-17T03:52:45-03:00
```

- Seats now record the deadline state *separately from later arrivals*, which is
  the specific behaviour r88 I2 asked for. `K1.md:8`:

```
- Kept the deadline state separate from the late arrival: at 03:52:42, only M1 and M3
  excerpt patches were observed; M4's four-excerpt patch first appeared at 03:54:02.
  M2 was not observed through the final 03:55:02 poll.
```

- The blocked-seat gaps collapsed. r88's blocked seats were 3m13s–17m09s late;
  r89's worst pre-deadline miss is `T1` waiting on `D3` for **1m02s**
  (`T1.md:12`: deadline listing `03:52:39`; `D3-r9-demos.patch` mtime `03:53:41`).

What did not work — the loop shape exceeds the tool cap by construction:

- 25 checks × 60 s = 1500 s in one foreground shell call, against a 600 s cap.
  Ten seats report the cap explicitly:

```
$ grep -rln "600-second\|600 second\|hit the.*cap" .ufo/waves/round-89/*.md | sed 's#.*/##' | tr '\n' ' '
C8.md D1.md E1.md F1.md K1.md M2.md M3.md M5.md R1.md X2.md
```

- `R1.md:237` records the resulting cadence hole:

```
There was an 82-second gap from poll 09 to poll 10 because the first process hit the
tool's 600-second cap; subsequent intervals were 60 or 61 seconds. Poll 25 was 23
seconds after the nominal deadline.
```

- `X1.md:9` records a 2m54s dead window when the loop died and no replacement was
  running:

```
The first poll process timed out after its 03:37:30 check; polling resumed at 03:40:24,
leaving that gap. The timestamped record is /tmp/lore-r89-X1-dependent-poll.log.
```

- The protocol only worked because seats noticed and restarted a second monitor
  (`K1.md:68`), so the receipt is partly reconstructed after the fact.
- Deadline convention is per-seat (start + 25 min), so the deadline itself is
  spread: cited values include `03:52:19`, `03:52:39`, `03:52:42`, `03:52:45`,
  `03:52:46`, `03:52:49`, `03:52:57`, `03:53:03`, `03:53:09`.

Outcome measurement (the mission's "did most seats make deadline"): the answer
depends on what is counted, and both readings are true.


- **Corrected attribution (v2):** two counts must not be conflated. The poll log's

  absent at that instant). The clerk's separate **03:52:53 inventory**
  (`W1.md:6,55`; landing-record table) counts **21**, the 21st being X1, whose two

## Handoff

### Three concrete improvements for r90

**I1 — Make the poll loop fit the tool cap, and make the cadence machine-checkable.**
Receipt: 25 × 60 s = 1500 s > 600 s cap; 10 seats report the cap
(`C8 D1 E1 F1 K1 M2 M3 M5 R1 X2`); `R1` an 82 s gap; `X1` a 2m54s dead window.
Form: poll in calls of at most 9 checks (9 × 60 s = 540 s, safely under the cap) or
launch the loop as a background task with a 1800 s timeout; require every dependent
