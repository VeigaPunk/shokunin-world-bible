## Answers to the three questions asked

**1. Within the candidate family?** Yes on content for the two C1 lines. `:195` and `:250` carry every element E1's candidates carry except the qualifier: the record label, both unassessed comparisons ("provenance continuity and tick-order comparisons"), the ambiguous holder ID as `field 'id'`, and the origin record label. On one axis the landed wording is **better than the whole family**: E1's three templates interpolate a single `holder_id`, so under two-field ambiguity they would print only one of the two fields; the landed `cause` join prints both, verified in the both-holders scratch case and independently documented at `corpus/knowledge/holder-assessment-boundary.md` item 3 ("joined by `, ` in the order `to_holder` then `from_holder`"). `:243` is **not** from the family — it is the pre-C1 string verbatim (identical to `E1.md`'s baseline transcript), and E1's candidates were all written to replace or extend that notice rather than to coexist with it. The qualifier difference is a scope difference rather than a missing family element; see the mechanism note below.

**2. Consistent in voice?** Only partly, and this is where the landed choice costs something.
- Consistent: the noun phrase "provenance continuity and tick-order comparisons" is identical across `:195`/`:243`/`:250`, and each line follows the checker's dominant `{label}: {condition}` shape.
- Inconsistent construction: one output block mixes two templates. `:243` is a bare passives clause with no ID; `:195`/`:250` are `because <cause> first occurred at <label>`. At the origin record both fire, so a single record gets two near-identical disclosures (measured: 4 disclosure lines for 3 provenance records in both masked fixtures, with `entities[3].provenance[0]` labelled twice).
- Tautology at the origin: `:250` says "first occurred at `{record_label}`" while being attached to that very record, so the clause is self-referential exactly where `:243` has already said "at this and all later records".
- Missing determiner: "because ambiguous holder identity from_holder 'dup' first occurred" reads as headlinese; and "occurred" is predicated of an *identity*, where what actually occurred is a record's *reference* to a duplicated ID.
- The `input-structural finding, not sourced-canon` qualifier is absent from all three disclosure lines while the sibling provenance findings at `:267`/`:269`/`:271` carry it. In the two delivered masked fixtures the disclosure lines are the *only* provenance findings emitted, so those outputs contain no structural/canon qualification at all, and the `E2c` scratch case shows the two voices side by side in one block. The omission is **defensible on mechanism grounds** (next section); what it costs is reader-level asymmetry in the delivered output.
- Checker-wide: the file already has a reason-after-colon idiom for unassessability at `:128` (`cannot assess event {event_id!r}: timeline.json unavailable`). The disclosure uses "unassessed because …" instead. E1's candidates shared this, so it is pre-existing, not introduced by C1.

### Why the missing qualifier is defensible — and where E1's candidates over-generalize

The qualifier at `:267`/`:269`/`:271` is not unconditional. It is gated on `chain_has_ambiguous_source`, set only when a record's `source_id` resolves to two or more source rows (`:258-259`). Its real semantic is therefore "this chain finding rests on a citation whose source is ambiguous, so it is an input-structural finding rather than a sourced-canon claim". Holder ambiguity is a different mechanism — a duplicated **entity** id — and the disclosure that follows makes no sourced-canon claim at all: it reports only that two comparisons were skipped. E1's three candidates attached the phrase to the holder-ambiguity disclosure anyway, justifying it only by "existing provenance-chain findings" using it, without distinguishing the two mechanisms. `corpus/knowledge/holder-assessment-boundary.md:102` (seat I2, refreshed in this wave) draws exactly that distinction, and in the opposite direction: "an ambiguous source citation earlier in the chain prefixes `input-structural finding, not sourced-canon; ` — ambiguous SOURCE, a different mechanism from ambiguous holder identity". So the landed omission is neither a regression against the baseline nor obviously wrong; it is E1's recommendation that would need justification.

**3. Free of overclaim?** Yes — no landed line asserts a chain break, a satisfied chain, or any canon fact; every one says "unassessed". Two low-risk residual misreadings remain, both of which E1 had already flagged as risk classes for its own candidates:
- `:243`'s "at this and all later records" is unanchored to the provenance array, so it could be read as document-wide rather than per-item. E1 flagged the same class for Candidate 1's "from … onward".
- `:195`/`:250`'s "first occurred at" is event-like and is the risk E1 flagged for Candidate 3's "was ambiguous at"; "occurred" is marginally stronger than "was". The risk is bounded because the origin is a structural path (`entities[3].provenance[0]`), not a tick, and the cause is labelled "ambiguous holder identity".

## Polish debt (future round material — NOT this round's claim)

E1's Candidate 1, adapted by substituting the landed `{cause}` join for its single `{holder_id!r}`, is **strictly better than the landed wording on voice uniformity**: one template for every record from the origin onward removes the origin-record duplicate, removes the tautological "first occurred at `<this record>`", and keeps the latched "onward" scope, with no change to content or multi-field coverage:

```python
f'{record_label}: provenance continuity and tick-order comparisons are unassessed from {origin_record_label} onward because {cause} is ambiguous'
```

Whether to also prefix E1's `input-structural finding, not sourced-canon;` is a **judgment call, not a strict win**: adding it buys reader uniformity with the sibling findings but imports E1's over-generalization of a phrase that currently marks ambiguous-source chains only. Candidate 3, adapted the same way, is a close second and has the milder "was ambiguous at" tense, at the cost of a parenthetically placed qualifier. Both remain unapplied; neither is verified as an end-to-end fixture change.

Two further wording debts, independent of template choice:
- `:243`'s "all later records" should name its scope (e.g. "later records of this provenance array") to close the document-wide misreading.
- The checker has two idioms for the same semantic ("unassessed because …" vs. `cannot assess …: <reason>`); unifying them is a cross-round voice task, not a C1 defect.
