### 2. Q — did the 25-minute poll protocol work?

**Verdict: improved in design, broken in implementation.**

What worked:

- The r88 I2 ask ("calibrated, timestamped deadline") was adopted with the right
  number: r88's measured last-author latency was +24m03s, and r89 set 25 minutes.
- The clerk executed it exactly as specified. The poll log's first and last checks
  bracket the window to the second:

```
$ python3 - <<'EOF'   # parse /tmp/W1-round89-polls-corrected.log
... polls: 25 first 2026-10-17T03:28:45-03:00 last 2026-10-17T03:52:45-03:00
```

- Seats now record the deadline state *separately from later arrivals*, which is
  the specific behaviour r88 I2 asked for. `K1.md:8`:

```
- Kept the deadline state separate from the late arrival: at 03:52:42, only M1 and M3
  excerpt patches were observed; M4's four-excerpt patch first appeared at 03:54:02.
  M2 was not observed through the final 03:55:02 poll.
```

- The blocked-seat gaps collapsed. r88's blocked seats were 3m13s–17m09s late;
  r89's worst pre-deadline miss is `T1` waiting on `D3` for **1m02s**
  (`T1.md:12`: deadline listing `03:52:39`; `D3-r9-demos.patch` mtime `03:53:41`).

What did not work — the loop shape exceeds the tool cap by construction:

- 25 checks × 60 s = 1500 s in one foreground shell call, against a 600 s cap.
  Ten seats report the cap explicitly:

```
$ grep -rln "600-second\|600 second\|hit the.*cap" .ufo/waves/round-89/*.md | sed 's#.*/##' | tr '\n' ' '
C8.md D1.md E1.md F1.md K1.md M2.md M3.md M5.md R1.md X2.md
```

- `R1.md:237` records the resulting cadence hole:

```
There was an 82-second gap from poll 09 to poll 10 because the first process hit the
tool's 600-second cap; subsequent intervals were 60 or 61 seconds. Poll 25 was 23
seconds after the nominal deadline.
```

- `X1.md:9` records a 2m54s dead window when the loop died and no replacement was
  running:

```
The first poll process timed out after its 03:37:30 check; polling resumed at 03:40:24,
leaving that gap. The timestamped record is /tmp/lore-r89-X1-dependent-poll.log.
```

- The protocol only worked because seats noticed and restarted a second monitor
  (`K1.md:68`), so the receipt is partly reconstructed after the fact.
- Deadline convention is per-seat (start + 25 min), so the deadline itself is
  spread: cited values include `03:52:19`, `03:52:39`, `03:52:42`, `03:52:45`,
  `03:52:46`, `03:52:49`, `03:52:57`, `03:53:03`, `03:53:09`.

Outcome measurement (the mission's "did most seats make deadline"): the answer
depends on what is counted, and both readings are true.

```
$ python3 - <<'EOF'   # per-seat first/last artifact mtime vs deadline 03:52:57
seats with first artifact <= deadline: 19/32
seats with last artifact  >  deadline: 23/32
   -> S2 S3 X1 X2 X3 F1 F2 C5 C6 C8 M2 M3 M4 M5 C7 K1 D2 T1 R1 D3 E1 W1 W2
seats with NO artifact at all: []
EOF
```

- By the clerk's own poll log, 21 of 31 non-clerk seats had at least one artifact
  at the 03:52:53 cutoff — matching the landing record's claim exactly. That is
  the claim "most seats made deadline" and it verifies.
- But 23 of 32 seats wrote again after the deadline, and 19 of 32 seats' final
  artifacts (by current mtime) are pre-deadline. So the deadline bounded *first
  contact*, not completion; the wave ran ~22 minutes past it.

## Findings & named debt

- **The 25-minute poll protocol is the round's clearest process improvement and it
  is implemented in a way that cannot run to completion.** The deadline is
  calibrated to r88's own latency, timestamped, and seats now separate deadline
  state from late arrivals; but 25 × 60 s = 1500 s in one shell call against a
  600 s cap means the loop dies by construction. Ten seats hit the cap, `R1`
  records an 82 s cadence gap, and `X1` lost 2m54s of monitoring. The protocol
  survives on seat improvisation, not on the instruction.
- **The deadline bounded first contact, not completion.** 21 of 31 non-clerk seats
  had an artifact at the 03:52:53 cutoff (the landing record is accurate), but 23
  of 32 seats wrote after it, and the round's headline artifacts are among the
  late ones (`X1.md` 03:57:53, `C5.md` 03:58:27, `C8.md` 03:59:05, `C6.md` 04:02:21).
- **Roster-as-data improved to the point of reconciling, and stopped there.**
  32 declared = 32 delivered is a real gain over r88's 35/31/37, and the `phase`
  field exists; but the roster is still Markdown, carries no target hash, and
  misses the one integrator-authored fixture. The r88 I3 defect recurred in
  miniature.
- **The landing record is the only surviving record of the cutoff state.** It is
  accurate and shipped in the commit, but two seats overwrote their pre-cutoff
  artifacts in place, so the cutoff is not reproducible from the tree (19/32 seats
  reconstructible vs 21 observed).
- **Freeze-then-verify held on the artifact the debt named.** The delivered checker
  hash equals the phase-1-verified hash (`e2bb9639…`), which is the direct repair
  of r88's `dcacffd…` ≠ `efe5810…` defect. What remains is smaller and different:
  three integrator edits (new fixture, composed table, one map field) each
  unverified before the commit, each disclosed in-artifact or in the commit
  message, and no `AMEND` artifact.
- **Named debt: a delivered artifact misnames its source patch.**
  `S2-fact-map.json`'s `anchor_basis` still points at `M3-excerpts.patch` with
  status "not applied to the cluster" while the delivered excerpt bytes are M4's.
  The anchors resolve (14/14), so this is a provenance defect, not a support defect.
- **Named debt: one reviewer recommendation was overridden without a wave-visible
  reason.** `C7` preferred M3's shell-charter revision; M4's landed. The reason is
  in the commit message only.
- **Carried, not repaired:** no machine-readable spec (r86's JSON pair still
  unused); no target-hash column; `/tmp` poll logs are ephemeral, so 5 of the 8
  logs left behind have zero timestamped lines and the durable evidence is each
  report's own prose.

## Handoff

### Three concrete improvements for r90

**I1 — Make the poll loop fit the tool cap, and make the cadence machine-checkable.**
Receipt: 25 × 60 s = 1500 s > 600 s cap; 10 seats report the cap
(`C8 D1 E1 F1 K1 M2 M3 M5 R1 X2`); `R1` an 82 s gap; `X1` a 2m54s dead window.
Form: poll in calls of at most 9 checks (9 × 60 s = 540 s, safely under the cap) or
launch the loop as a background task with a 1800 s timeout; require every dependent
seat to close its report with one line —
`POLLS <n> SPAN <first-ISO>..<last-ISO> GAPS <n>` — so the clerk can verify the
cadence from the report instead of reconstructing it from `/tmp`.

**I2 — Make the landing record a hash-stamped freeze, not an mtime inventory.**
Receipt: 23 of 32 seats wrote after the cutoff, and E1/M3 overwrote pre-cutoff
files in place, so only 19 of 32 seats' cutoff state survives on the tree.
Form: at the cutoff the clerk writes `landing-<ts>.json` with
`path`, `sha256`, `mtime` per artifact (read-only; no stash), and the follow-up
wave diffs delivered-vs-cutoff by hash. This turns "what existed at the mark" into
a claim that survives in-place rewrites.

**I3 — Publish the roster and the amendment log as data (adopt r86's JSON form plus
r88's optional I4).** Receipt: r89 reconciled the count (32/32) yet the roster
still misses the integrator-authored fixture, and the three integrator edits have
no artifact; `find .ufo -name 'wave-spec.json' -o -name 'wave-validation.json'` →
r86 only. Form: `wave-spec.json` with `seat / phase / claim / target_hash` rows and
an `amendments[]` array (`artifact`, `before_sha256`, `after_sha256`, `closes`,
`author`), plus `wave-validation.json` asserting declared == delivered and that
every amendment hash is present in the delivered tree. The r89 edits are exactly
the rows this would have carried: the `r89-c1-malformed-source-id` fixture, the
composed decision table, and the `S2-fact-map.json` R3 anchor rewrite.

*Optional fourth:* fix the deadline convention. Per-seat start + 25 min produced
cited deadlines from `03:52:19` to `03:53:09`, which is why the clerk's single
cutoff matches no seat's own deadline exactly. One wave-wide timestamp (published
in the spec) would make "the deadline state" a single checkable instant.

### Gaps and limits of this audit

- I judged process, not the axis claims; the per-axis verdicts belong to the axis
  seats. My §6 measurements are evidence inputs, not verdicts.
- The reconstruction used the chosen artifacts (`X1 F1 F2 F3 D3 M4 M5`); `M1` and
  `M3` are alternatives that conflict by construction, so "the delivered tree
  differs from M3's patch" is not an amendment finding.
- `/tmp` poll logs are ephemeral and format-inconsistent (only 3 of 8 carry
  timestamped lines); I relied on each report's own timestamped text, which is
  self-reported and unverifiable except where artifact mtimes corroborate it.
- The `r89-c1-malformed-source-id` fixture was replayed read-only; I did not
  assess whether it closes X1's debt adequately (that is C1's seat).
- Only this file was written. No commit, push, registry, or sibling-repo write.
