# D2 — Independent C3 governance decisions

**Status:** independent decision-table proposal for R8–R10; not integrated canon or a checker change. Destination, if selected: append after R10 in `corpus/knowledge/r88-sourced-inspiration-decision-table.md`, preserving its six-column table.

**Axes:** governance completeness; source/referent identity calibration; traceability of owners, triggers, and outcomes.

| Decision | Rule / output | Attribution | Rights status | AI-training permission | Owner, trigger, worked example, and integration check |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **R8 — A general motif has a plausible but unrecorded influence claim** | A broad resemblance alone does not establish influence or a source lineage. A named-source claim, creator statement of consultation, surviving draft/research record, or source-specific combination/sequence beyond a common motif triggers review; the integrating lore editor asks the author for a short, non-expressive provenance note naming the source/locator, affected element, and design effect. On opening the review, record a dated neutral `possible influence: unverified` note in the inspiration note/map (create the minimal record if absent); this records a question, not a source-use finding. If the author confirms no particular source materially informed the element and the integration editor finds no contrary source-specific evidence and only a commonplace motif remains, record the response and basis and remove the provisional note before applying R2. If use is confirmed, apply R1 and record the source; if only a general motif remains and no distinctive expression/sequence carries through, the element may remain as newly authored fiction with `external-only / unverified inspiration` in the note/map, delivery status in the ledger, and independent in-world support for each canon claim. If the influence question is unanswered or materiality unresolved, do not convert silence into R2: log the missing evidence in `l0-questions.md` and withhold the source-dependent detail from supported canon pending review. | Name a supported work and consulted edition/version/locator when known. Keep a creator's influence statement separate from a verified passage claim; a motif resemblance is not a source citation. | Keep open unless evidence establishes status for the specific work/expression; this is an editorial screen, not a legal finding or rights grant. | Keep separately open unless explicit scoped terms establish permission; no influence or attribution decision implies permission. | **Owner:** integrating lore editor opens the review; author supplies provenance; unresolved cases go to the operator through `l0-questions.md`. **Trigger:** evidence beyond generic similarity, or an asserted but undocumented consultation. **Example:** a draft uses a low-water crossing, a widespread motif, with no source-specific detail and no record of consultation. Similarity alone does not create a source claim; ask the author. A confirmed no-source/no-lineage account plus the editor's finding of no contrary source-specific evidence and only a commonplace motif permits R2. If the author says a named tale informed the design but cannot identify what carried through, record an open influence question and hold the dependent detail rather than silently calling it original. At integration inspect the note, element map, ledger, and independent in-world claim support. |
| **R9 — A translation names its translator but its edition/version is unknown** | Keep the honest delivery mode `translation`; record the translator, named source work, `version: unknown`, excerpt path/lines, and the open bibliographic field. An unknown version does not by itself disqualify support when a reliable citation or bibliographic record ties the delivered translation by that translator to the named source work and the excerpt is anchored and manually reviewed: it can support only the claim as expressed in that identified translation, never the source's exact original wording or an edition-specific variant. If only the translator is named, or the link to a named source work cannot be verified, translator identity alone is insufficient: record `delivered translation; source/version link unverified`, fail R5 support for the source-attributed claim, and re-source or rephrase it. Do not mislabel a delivered translation as a paraphrase or say the translation itself was not delivered. | Attribute eligible wording to the named translator's rendering of the named work. Keep source-language wording and any unverified edition/version distinct. | Review rights for the delivered translation and edition separately; unknown version leaves scope open and does not imply clearance. | Assess separately for the delivered expression; translator attribution and delivery mode imply no permission. | **Owner:** excerpt/map author records metadata; integrating lore editor verifies the bibliographic tie and support status. **Trigger:** any `translation` entry whose version/edition is unknown. **Example:** a delivered line is attributed to M. A. Translator and *The River Codex*; a reliable catalogue entry ties that translator's rendering and passage to that work, but the printing is not recorded. Mark the printing unknown; the line may support “the translation says the keeper set the ring on the fourth stair,” not a claim about the original wording. If no source-work link can be substantiated, leave the passage delivered but its source attribution unsupported. At integration check the tie, line anchor, whole-claim support, and the map's explicit unknown-version status. |
| **R10 — Two clusters share a referent name but describe different occasions** | A repeated display name is a comparison trigger, not proof of shared identity; local entity/event IDs, matching ticks, and real-world parallels do not establish a cross-cluster referent. Require cited in-world evidence for identity and for the relevant occasion. Even when identity is established, claims about distinct, supported occasions are not a contradiction unless they assert incompatible values for the same field of the same occasion. If identity or occasion remains unestablished, record a candidate discrepancy and the missing evidence in the incoming cluster's README under `## R10 comparison outcomes` and in `l0-questions.md`, not in the confirmed-conflict register. For any compared-and-dismissed overlap, record both cluster paths and claim pointers, supported occasion/scope, and the rationale in that README section; this is an audit trail, not a conflict entry. Add to `corpus/knowledge/cross-cluster-contradictions.md` only when both source-backed claims share a demonstrated referent, field, and occasion and cannot both be true as stated; preserve both claims as unresolved. | Retain each cluster's own in-world source and locator. An external parallel cannot identify a fictional referent or adjudicate canon. | Rights status cannot decide identity or canon. | Training permission cannot decide identity or canon; assess independently. | **Owner:** integrating lore editor performs the manual cross-cluster comparison; cluster authors supply identity/occasion evidence; the register reviewer verifies only confirmed pairs. **Trigger:** overlapping names/claims or an explicit cross-reference between landed clusters. **Example (hypothetical, not corpus canon):** both clusters mention “Ema”; cited accounts establish the same Ema, but one places her at the east gate at dawn in year 2 and the other at Reed Arch at dusk in year 4. These are different occasions, so no R10 entry is made; log the compared pointers, occasion evidence, and rationale in the incoming README's R10 comparison outcomes section. If the shared name has no identity evidence, record only a candidate and what would resolve it there and in `l0-questions.md`. At integration check the two pointers and sources, and keep the live register empty unless a confirmed incompatible pair exists. |

## Integration boundary

These are governance decisions, not automated verification. A clean per-cluster checker run cannot establish excerpt fidelity, historical influence, cross-cluster identity, or canon. The worked examples above are illustrative only; do not add them to the live cross-cluster register or treat them as corpus facts. The D2 patch changes no tracked corpus file.
