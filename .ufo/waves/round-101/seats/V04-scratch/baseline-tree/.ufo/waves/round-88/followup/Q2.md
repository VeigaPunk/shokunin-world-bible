### 2. Q — did the persisted wave-spec help?

**Verdict: improved, but not yet auditable.** Real gains over r87:

- It pins the baseline (`dfd1fbc`), the three claims, and 31 seat names, and all
  37 reports cite it. r87 had no spec at all, so Q4 could not check the roster or
  the seat count against any plan; r88 can.
- It pre-commits the three axes and the C1/C2/C3 after-states, which is what let
  `B1/B2/B3` establish a shared 145-tuple baseline (`SHA-256
  76cf55c0cdce35c538bb869033e1c9642c92901f650304ce547328aec2efb106` for both
  `B1-tuples.json` and `B2-tuples.json`) that four later seats compared against.

But the spec does not close the r87 gap it was written to close:

- The declared count (35) matches neither the enumerated roster (31) nor the
  delivered set (37), so "was the seat count justified" is still not answerable
  from the plan. The 6 seats that carried the round's decisive work are the ones
  missing from it.
- It is prose only. r86's machine-readable `wave-spec.json` +
  `wave-validation.json` pair would have made the count/roster check mechanical;
  r88's spec cannot be validated by a tool.

### 3. Q — did the author→verifier barrier eliminate the V2/T1/R1 collisions?

**Verdict: not eliminated in phase 1; eliminated in consequence by the
supplementary batch.** Split into three parts.

**(a) The barrier as declared was carved out for the exact seat that collided.**
`wave-spec.md:55-60` states the barrier and then exempts T1:

```
55:## Author→verifier barrier (Q4 P1)
58:verification happens in phase 2 against the DELIVERED tree; phase-1
59:same-wave verifiers were r87's collision source and are intentionally absent
60:here except critics (design-level) and T1 (cross-draft).
```

T1 is r87's collided role (§2.2 of Q4: "T1 / D1 — same defect, larger gap"). In
r88 T1 repeated it: its two prescribed checks ran at `02:22:18` and `02:23:28`
and found no D1/D2; `patches/D1-table.md` landed at `02:42:10`, i.e. **18m42s
after T1's second check**. T1 only produced a real verdict by reading the drafts
30 minutes later, self-labelled as a late read.

**(b) R1 is a literal recurrence of the r87 T1/D1 collision.**

## Findings & named debt

- **The wave-spec is the round's clearest process improvement and its clearest
  unfinished one.** It exists, pins the baseline, and was used by every seat; it
  is also internally inconsistent (35 vs 31 vs 37) and prose-only, so the seat
  count is still unauditable — the same finding as r87 §3, reduced but not closed.
- **The barrier was declared, then exempted for the one role that collided.**
  T1 stayed in phase 1 by design and missed its target by 18m42s; R1 repeated
  r87's T1/D1 block at +17m09s. The 60 s window was reduced rather than
  recalibrated, against r87 P1's explicit recommendation. 54% of reports declare
  a missed check.
- **The supplementary batch is what actually saved the round, and it is
  invisible in the plan.** Six undocumented same-model late seats produced the
  C3 selection, the C1 falsification that forced the delivered amendment, two
  independent censuses, the fixture verification, and the C2 compliance audit.
  Any r89 audit of "why did r88 converge" has to reconstruct it from mtimes.
- **New named debt: integrator amendments are outside the verification loop.**
  The delivered checker (`dcacffd…`) differs from the only verified artifact
  (`efe5810…`), and the fixture README expectation was rewritten in the same
  commit. No phase-1 seat saw the shipped revision; V2 in phase 2 did.
- **Carried, not repaired:** `E1`'s wording panel never saw X1's actual wording
  (`E1.md:7`), and the shipped wording is the amended one E1 never reviewed —
  the one miss the supplementary batch did not cover. `K1` fell back to sibling
  province docs because `S3.md` landed 3m13s after its check. The
  kotodama→lore handoff contract
  (`kotodama-shokunin/corpus/knowledge/synthesis/worldbible-integration-contract.md`)
  is still absent, blocking procedural conformance for C2 (`S3.md:10,26`,
  `K1.md:39`, `C7B.md:204`).
- **Census redundancy persists.** r87 Q4's "cap full-corpus replays at two" was
  not adopted: r88 ran four full 145-tuple sweeps (`B1`, `B2` baseline; `C6B`,
  `V1` post-fix) plus `C6`'s 154-directory sweep. The two post-fix sweeps are
  independent verification of X1 and defensible; the baseline pair is still one
  result twice.
- **Fixed, worth keeping:** the r87 "stale read asserted as current" defect did
  not recur — r88's availability claims are time-labelled or explicitly marked
  as point-in-time snapshots. And the r87 "ranker overridden with no reason"
  defect did not recur: R1B's D1 recommendation is what landed.

## Handoff

### Three concrete improvements for r89

**I1 — Freeze the delivered revision and re-verify it; void any PASS whose
artifact hash changed.** After the last patch is applied and before the commit,
re-run the verifier set against the frozen revision hash, and make "the verified
hash equals the delivered hash" a required line in the round record. Receipt for
the need: delivered `dcacffd…` vs verified `efe5810…`; the amendment and the
fixture README rewrite both landed after every verifier wrote. Cost is near zero
— `V1`/`C6B`/`J1` harnesses are reusable and V2 already proves the re-run
succeeds — and it converts the round's only unverified claim into a verified one.

**I2 — Replace the fixed 60 s check with either a real dependency barrier or a
calibrated, timestamped deadline.** Two acceptable forms: (a) do not dispatch a
dependent seat until its target's path+hash exists (the barrier the spec
declares but does not enforce); or (b) if one batch is required, set the window
from the previous round's measured latency and require the check to print
`date -Is`. r88's own latency data gives the number: last author artifact at
+24m03s from the spec, blocked-seat gaps 3m13s–17m09s; 60 s is ~10–24× short, and
54% of reports record a miss. Only `T1` timestamped its checks; make that
mandatory so "absent" is checkable against the report's own write time.

**I3 — Publish the roster as data, including the supplementary batch, with its
dispatch trigger and the hash it read.** r88's six rescue seats carried the
round's decisive work and appear in no manifest, count, or plan; the spec's own
count is self-contradictory (35 declared / 31 named / 37 delivered). r89 should
reuse r86's machine-readable form (`wave-spec.json` + `wave-validation.json`) and
extend it with a `phase` field (`work` | `supplementary` | `judge`) plus the
target hash per seat, so the seat count, the roster, and the
"which revision did this seat verify" question are all mechanically answerable.

*Optional fourth:* when the integrator amends an author artifact to close a
critic's falsification, record the amendment as a seat-visible artifact
(`AMEND-*.md` with before/after hashes and the falsification it closes). C5B's
finding currently reaches the delivered artifact only through the commit message;
an amendment artifact would let phase 2 audit the critic→amendment loop instead
of inferring it from hashes.

### Gaps and limits of this audit

- I judged process, not the axis claims; the per-axis verdicts belong to the
  axis seats. My delivered-tree measurements (§6) are evidence inputs, not
  verdicts.
- The 20-seat "declared a missed check" count comes from each report's own text
  and mtimes; there is no dispatch log, so dispatch order is inferred from
  artifact mtimes. If a seat was dispatched later than the wave start, its
  "missed" label may be an artifact of my assumption — the direction of every
  quoted gap is nonetheless confirmed by the artifact mtimes.
- Sibling follow-up seats (`V2`, `JDEP2`, `JCOV5`) landed while I read; I used
  `V2` because it verifies the delivered revision, and cite it as another seat's
  run. Later follow-up seats may add evidence this report does not reflect.
- Only this file was written. No commit, push, registry, or sibling-repo write.
