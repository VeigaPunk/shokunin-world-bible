## 2. Craft value: does the map add anything beyond restating fields?

It does, and the value is concentrated in one structural choice: **a per-clause `Limit` column that records
what the verse does not license.** Enumerated, with the evidence each claim rests on:

1. **It converts one global disclaimer into seven localized non-entailments.** `sources.json:9-11` gives the
   hymn a single `reliability_note` ("its imagery is not an objective account of supernatural events"). The
   map keeps that stance but attaches a distinct prohibition to each image. The clause `the shell ring`
   carries "Not a legal seal, faction emblem, or proof of alliance"; `pass the reed-bell hand to hand` carries
   "Does not prove that the Shellweavers or Reedwardens jointly handled the fixture's Reed Bell". Both are
   aimed at specific elements that really do exist in the fixture (`relations.json` `allied_with` edges,
   `entities.json` `reed-bell`) and whose real source is the charter, not the poem. That is targeted
   anti-laundering, not a restatement.
2. **It keeps the three epistemic tiers apart in one place.** Sourced canon (`*.json`, checker-validated),
   invented attributed verse (`lowwater-hymn-excerpt.md`), and editorial reading (the map) are separated, and
   the map's last line states its own tier ("interpretive readings of authored words, not new canon"). This
   matches the README's stated design.
3. **It preserves the verse/entity naming distinction.** The map quotes the verse form `reed-bell` while its
   limit names the fixture entity `Reed Bell`. A reader can see which string is poetry and which is an entity
   name, which is exactly the fusion the limit forbids.
4. **Hedge calibration is consistent and mostly correct.** Six of seven readings are hedged ("A possible
   image of…", "may mark", "A reading of…"). The seventh, `At low water` → "A moment of exposure or
   transition in this poem", is declarative but self-scoped to the poem, so it does not reach into canon.
   The strongest limit is the last: "Does not assert supernatural return, causation, or an objective event" —
   it names the three failure modes (supernatural, causal, factual) rather than gesturing at "interpretation".

What it does **not** add: no clause is linked to a fixture element by ID, so the map cannot be
machine-cross-checked against `entities.json` / `relations.json` / `timeline.json`. The limits are prose
targets a human must match to the right object. Section 3 shows the cost of that unenforced seam.

## 3. Defect found (named, not scored)

**Divergence: `entities.json` asserts a hymn-sourced claim the delivered hymn does not support, and the
map's own limit forbids exactly that assertion.**

`entities.json` (`mother-tide-gate`) contains:

```
"first_appearance": "lowwater-hymn",
"claims": [
  { "statement": "The Low-Water Hymn names the Mother-Tide Gate as a shore threshold.",
    "source_id": "lowwater-hymn" }
]
```

But the excerpt never names the gate, nor any shore/threshold/basin/vow:

```sh
cd corpus/fixtures/r87-myth-cluster
grep -c "Mother-Tide" lowwater-hymn-excerpt.md          # 0
grep -in "gate\|threshold\|shore\|basin\|vow" lowwater-hymn-excerpt.md   # no output, rc=1
grep -rln "Mother-Tide" .        # ./README.md ./entities.json ./sources.json ./timeline.json
```

The hymn's whole content is low water, a shell ring, a bowl, a tide, a reed-bell, water returning. The
`mother-tide-gate` place is genuinely attested elsewhere (`timeline.json` events `first-lowwater-signal` and
`moon-basin-vow-gate-account`, both sourced to `gate-witness`), so the place is not unsupported as such — the
*hymn attribution* is. Meanwhile the map's limit for `beside the bowl` states "No entity ID or physical
artifact is asserted by the poem", which is the correct reading and directly contradicts `entities.json`.

Status: **not a checker violation** (the checker does not read `.md`, verified above, and it does not judge
claim text against prose), and not an in-fixture contradiction of the reported-not-resolved kind. It is a
support gap in one JSON claim that the map's own limit column predicts. I did not repair it: no tracked file
is authorized to this seat, and the repair is a world-bible edit that belongs to whoever owns the fixture.

## 4. Strengths to reuse (named)

- `S1 — limit-per-image`: one explicit non-entailment per quoted image, each aimed at a real fixture element
  that a reader could wrongly fuse with the verse.
- `S2 — self-declared tier`: the map states its own epistemic status and that the checker ignores it, and
  that statement is verifiably true.
- `S3 — verbatim clause binding`: every mapped clause is a literal substring of the source; zero paraphrase
  drift. Cheap to verify mechanically.
- `S4 — naming-level discrimination`: verse spelling vs entity name kept distinct (`reed-bell` / `Reed Bell`).
- `S5 — hedge discipline with one deliberate exception`: six hedged readings, one declarative reading that is
  scoped to the poem itself rather than to canon.

## 5. Weaknesses to name (with evidence)

- `W1 — prose-only enforcement`: the limits are not machine-linked to IDs; the map cannot be cross-checked by
  the checker, and section 3 shows the seam leaked in the same fixture.
- `W2 — one low-information reading`: `At low water` → "A moment of exposure or transition in this poem" is
  close to a restatement of the phrase; it names no specific risk, unlike its six siblings.
- `W3 — no clause→element index`: no column ties a clause to the entity/relation/event it could contaminate
  (`the shell ring`→`allied_with`, `reed-bell`→`entities.json#reed-bell`). A reader must reconstruct the
  target; W1 is the same defect seen from the other side.
- `W4 — coverage of the non-verse frame`: the map quotes only the three verse lines. The excerpt's own
  framing sentence ("attributed fixture text, not an authenticated ritual or objective world account") does
  the same work as the map's limits and is not referenced by the map, so the two disclaimers can drift
  independently.
