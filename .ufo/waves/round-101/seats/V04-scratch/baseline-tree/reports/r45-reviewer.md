# Round 45 non-author reviewer — faction occupancy

## Verdict: NO-GO

Do not exempt faction-kind timeline actors from same-ID/tick, distinct-place `double-booked` checks on the current evidence. The checker behavior is observable and the concern is plausible, but no existing project ontology establishes that a faction subject is distributed presence rather than a singular/focal-location actor. A `kind == faction` exception would suppress a finding for every faction, including a genuine conflicting pair whose rows assert one focal location. The paired character control does not bound that faction-specific risk.

## Evidence and contract boundary

- `corpus/fixtures/check_bible.py:7-13` permits `character`, `place`, `faction`, and `item`; timeline `entity_id` is a required string, not a character-only reference. At `:228-252`, after actor identity/place/tick checks, occupancy is keyed by `(entity_id, tick)` for any uniquely indexed actor; a different place yields `double-booked`. There is no faction branch.
- `corpus/fixtures/README.md` explicitly says: “A character cannot occupy different places at the same tick; simultaneous records at the same place are allowed.” This is positive contract for character occupancy. It neither says every entity kind is singularly located nor says factions are exempt. It cannot be reversed into a blanket noncharacter permission. The README's faction discussion (`:9-27`) is narrowly about reciprocal `allied_with` records, and explicitly frames that as a chosen data convention, not an ontology of faction location.
- The faction scout's suggested report that the Reed Compact kept harbor and tower outposts staffed is fixture-authored prose. At most, under that input's chosen reading, it says the fictional report describes two staffed outposts. It is not authenticated, does not establish canon, and cannot independently establish what a generic timeline `entity_id` means. Nor does it establish that every faction event denotes distributed membership rather than the faction's headquarters, a unit/delegate, or another focal location. Source ID resolution and checker acceptance/findings are structural diagnostics, not truth or semantic adjudication.

## Distributed collective versus singular location

A distributed-outpost interpretation is coherent: one group may staff separate posts at the same local watch, so two rows naming the faction could be compatible. But the checker/schema does not represent a member, unit, outpost-presence subtype, or distributed-location relation; it sees the same unique faction ID and two place IDs. A singular/focal interpretation is equally compatible with the current representation: the row can attribute an event to a faction as one actor/location, and distinct places at the same tick can then be inconsistent. The kind label alone cannot choose between these readings. A blanket faction exclusion chooses the first for all cases without evidence.

R12 concerns duplicate versus unique actor identity and retains a definite collision for a unique actor; it does not establish faction multiplicity. R21 isolates occupancy from malformed dependencies under unique actor/place/tick guards, not faction semantics. R22 changes dependency traversal while leaving occupancy alone. R24 handles ambiguous relation endpoints, not timeline occupancy. None supplies the missing faction-location contract. R44 is relevant in the opposite direction: generic `entity_id` and character-specific prose did not justify a character-only gate. That rejection does not imply faction immunity; generic actor typing permits the question but does not settle its semantics. R45 scout observations that faction and character pairs currently both trigger the diagnostic demonstrate implementation behavior only.

## Risks, axis judgment, falsifier

Calibration is **not shown to improve**: the proposed exemption could prevent an intended warning for singular/focal faction records. That is a credible harm, not merely an untested edge. The character control proves only that character behavior can remain; character control alone cannot protect faction conflict. Coverage and depth are unchanged. Preserve behavior and do not claim a new calibration gain.

Reconsider only if an independently grounded, project-level convention distinguishes distributed-presence faction events from focal-location faction events (or introduces an explicit scoped field/subtype), rather than deriving the distinction from kind or round-authored prose. Falsify the narrow proposal with both: (1) a supported distributed-outpost pair that currently produces the suspected false positive, and (2) a supported singular/focal-location conflict that must still be diagnosed. Also retain the unique-character conflict, check duplicate/invalid actor/place/tick guards and unrelated kinds, and replay the pinned old fixture set. If the same kind-level exemption suppresses the focal-location finding, or no existing convention can distinguish the cases without editorially assuming the desired outcome, NO-GO remains. A local tick is an editorial sequence value, not by itself proof of an absolute simultaneous instant.
