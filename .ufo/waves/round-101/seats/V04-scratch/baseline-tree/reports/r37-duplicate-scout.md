Its claim calls for a clean r35-derived control and a negative case duplicating `tarin-envelope-observation`; expected diagnostics include the duplicate event ID and `claim_a.event_id: expected exactly one timeline event` (`.shokunin/lore/LOG.md`, Round 37 declaration).

Thus a duplicate target is not silently resolved by the last-wins index for ledger validation; its endpoint fails unique resolution. The count is over schema-valid rows, since only rows passing required-field checks enter `data` (`check_bible.py:31-43`).
- Round 9 supplies a relevant precedent but tests a different consumer. Its paired fixtures swap two duplicate `same` timeline rows around a dependent event; the independent verifier reports identical duplicate-ID plus local ambiguous-dependency findings under either order, while baseline output had differed (`.shokunin/lore/reports/r9-fixture-scout.md`, “Proposed paired inputs” and “Probe evidence”; `.shokunin/lore/reports/r9-independent-verifier.md`, “Paired-fixture and probe evidence”). Round 9's checker change uses `event_id_counts` to avoid tick comparison when a dependency target is duplicated (`check_bible.py:257-274`); that does not itself test contradiction-ledger endpoints.
- The round-35 verifier establishes the unique-ID control's structural behavior: corrected r35 fixture exits 0, and the ledger is a manual, unresolved editorial pairing, not checker-proven semantic incompatibility (`.shokunin/lore/reports/r35-independent-verifier.md`, “Final r35 checker” and “What validation does and does not establish”).

## Risks and interpretation

- Duplicate event ID leaves the ledger endpoint ambiguous: the checker cannot establish that `claim_a` points to exactly one event assertion. The ledger diagnostic is in addition to the duplicate-ID finding; it is not a selection of the first/last row.
- Do not turn this into a claim that Tarin's or Sella's testimony is false, or that the Bell-7 account conflict is resolved. Both accounts remain narrative material; the duplicated input merely prevents a unique ledger endpoint. Even with unique pointers, checker success proves pointer/schema conditions only, not source prose, shared occasion, identity, truth, or canon resolution.
- The `expected exactly one` message applies to count zero as well as count greater than one; interpret it alongside the duplicate-ID finding to distinguish this fixture's ambiguity from a missing endpoint. A duplicate malformed row excluded from the valid timeline list would not raise the valid-ID count, whereas a schema-valid duplicate does.
- Each contradiction side is checked independently. A duplicated `claim_a` event should produce its side-local endpoint error while the unique `claim_b` still undergoes its own checks. Avoid assuming every later diagnostic is suppressed globally; report exact output from the actual pair.

This tests ledger pointer coverage only, not account truth or canon.
