# Round 79 adversarial reader — queue-order choice

## Finding: narrow GO, fiction-local only

The proposed excerpt does support one specific, observable editorial choice after stripping role/title, any slate linkage, and editor gloss: **show the names in arrival order rather than silently reorder them into the clerk’s call order.** The warrant is entirely in the two sentences: “I wrote the names in the order people reached my desk, not the order the clerk called them. If I copied the clerk’s order, the people who had waited longest would look as though they came last.” The first sentence names the competing orders; the second gives a concrete interpretive consequence for choosing one. No title, authority, slate, or outside inference is needed.

The no-excerpt counterfactual is clear: **without these sentences, this excerpt supplies no reason to choose arrival order over clerk order** (or to show a list at all). That absence is a real before/after distinction, not proof the choice is independently established. It also exposes the circularity risk: the excerpt is invented with the rationale already built in, and a citation ID would only point back to that authored rationale. Thus this supports an editorial decision about how to present this fictional scene; it does not independently establish a real queue, procedure, character trait, or setting fact. The logic is not circular if the bounded use is simply to preserve the expressly stated contrast and consequence in the scene. It becomes circular if the claim is inflated into “the source proves arrival order is the true/fair procedure” or if the author’s choice to invent the premise is offered as independent evidence that the premise merits canon or broader world treatment.

I judge this a **narrow depth GO** under the declared test: it is more than generic texture because it selects between two visible orderings and explains how they change what the reader sees about waiting. This is a modest narrative/editorial choice, not evidence of an important institution or a durable setting rule. A clean source-ID check, if one were later run, would establish only pointer resolution; it would not validate the prose, its truth, or its entailment. No claim here about source-text truth/authenticity, canon, rights, or game mechanics.

## Falsifier / boundary

Reverse this GO if a blind editor given only the excerpt and the two alternatives cannot identify arrival-order display as the choice, or finds no meaningful difference between the alternatives beyond restating the sentence. Also reject any broader claimed gain if it requires a title/authority, an editor’s added moral gloss, a slate/report connection, or treating the invented rationale as evidence of an actual procedure. The exact textual warrant for the narrow choice is the contrast “order people reached my desk, not the order the clerk called them” plus the stated consequence “people who had waited longest would look as though they came last.”
