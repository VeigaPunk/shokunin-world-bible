## Risks / boundary

Fictional-looking source identifiers (`tarin-note`, `archive-ledger`, `courier-log`) and a `source_id` citation are internal reference links, not authentication, chain of custody, independent corroboration, publication provenance, or copyright/licensing evidence. A source row may be unique and structurally resolvable while its prose is invented; checker success does not validate entailment. Likewise, calling an excerpt “newly authored” is a fixture statement, not an independent audit of authorship or a legal rights clearance. Do not infer ownership, license, reuse rights, or AI-training permission from fictional framing, public availability, attribution, or ID uniqueness. Keep those real-world questions distinct and unresolved unless separately evidenced.

The narrow near-miss risk is upgrading hearsay (“Tarin heard that Sella placed…”) to direct observation, while overcorrecting it as proof the placement did not happen. It supports only that the fictional text depicts Tarin reporting hearsay; it neither establishes nor disproves the underlying placement. Keep r33’s first-person “saw” as a character-reported observation, conditional on editorial ID alignment—not objective/canon fact. No contents, recipient, owner, motive, destination, Blue Seal/carbon/route-strip connection follows.
