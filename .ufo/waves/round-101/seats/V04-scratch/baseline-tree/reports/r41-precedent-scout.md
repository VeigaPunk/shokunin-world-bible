# Round 41 precedent scout — epistemic-strength novelty

## Assessment

The proposed one-clause change is materially distinct from r34 and r36 in the *type of source/record mismatch* it probes, but its calibration novelty is modest and conditional. R34 changes evidentiary modality (heard a proposition versus saw an act); r36 changes the claimed occasion/date and breaks the shared-occasion premise. R41 instead preserves auditory modality and the occasion wording while changing Vela's own identification from explicitly tentative to direct. The key issue is whether the fixed record's “took to be” wording is a faithful paraphrase, an underclaim, or logically compatible with the stronger excerpt—not whether an objective bell is proven.

## Precedent comparison

- R34's exact control says “I, Tarin, saw Sella place…” and its near-miss says “I, Tarin, heard that Sella placed…” (`.shokunin/lore/corpus/fixtures/r34-tarin-hearsay/tarin-note-excerpt.md:1`; `.shokunin/lore/corpus/fixtures/r34-tarin-hearsay/modality-contrast-map.md:8-14`). That contrast changes **how Tarin learned of the placement**: direct sight versus hearing an assertion. The map says the unchanged `reports seeing` record is unsupported by the hearsay excerpt but not thereby false (`.../modality-contrast-map.md:9-11`). R41 does not change direct/indirect modality: Vela still hears the strokes; it alters whether she presents the sound's identity tentatively or directly.
- R36 keeps the shared Bell-7 label but changes Sella's explicit date from the seventh to the eighth day while Tarin remains on the seventh (`.shokunin/lore/corpus/fixtures/r36-bell7-wrong-date/tarin-note-excerpt.md:1-3`; `.../sella-bell7-note-excerpt.md:1-3`). Its map identifies the resulting failure to establish one shared occasion and notes the copied JSON/ledger rationale remains seventh-day text unsupported by the changed excerpt (`.../date-contrast-map.md:5-8`; `.shokunin/lore/reports/r36-date-scout.md:12-19`). R41 holds the date/occasion clause fixed; it does not challenge cross-account synchronization.
- R40 already says Vela “heard two strokes I took to be the registry bell” and explicitly limits this to her tentative identification, not objective ringing (`.shokunin/lore/corpus/fixtures/r40-vela-listener/vela-note-excerpt.md:1`; `.shokunin/lore/corpus/fixtures/r40-vela-listener/vela-clause-map.md:9-16`). The proposed r41 edit (“heard the registry bell strike twice”) would strengthen Vela's *reported identification*. With the claim/event held at “she took to be,” there is a source-to-record **precision/asymmetry** to assess, not an objective contradiction. A stronger source statement entails or permits the weaker report as a compatible underclaim in ordinary logic; it does not establish that the JSON is false. Nor does the source establish objective bell origin merely by stating the identification directly.

## Risks and limits

1. Do not frame this as r34-style hearsay-versus-observation: “heard the registry bell strike twice” remains an auditory report, but unlike “heard that X happened” it does not grammatically make the bell claim hearsay.
2. Do not label the unchanged JSON as logically incompatible or false. It may be a weaker, compatible attribution; the narrower concern is that it fails to preserve the excerpt's stronger asserted identification / exact epistemic force.
3. Conversely, do not silently promote the record to objective ringing. First-person source text is still Vela's claim, not authentication; a citation ID and clean structural check do not prove it.
4. As a calibration candidate this is only a small manual source-to-record fidelity test. If the evaluation criterion is merely “does the excerpt allow this weaker statement?”, it may pass and show no mismatch. The claim needs a declared criterion about representing the source's stated confidence, not an assertion of entailment failure.

## Falsifiable check

Give non-authors the exact r40/r41 excerpts and the unchanged Vela claim/event, with the preferred interpretation withheld. Ask separately: (a) what does Vela's excerpt claim about identifying the sound; (b) what strength does the record encode; (c) is the record false/incompatible, merely weaker, or an exact rendition; and (d) does either version establish objective bell origin? Require quoted language. Expected bounded result: r40 is explicitly tentative (“I took to be”); proposed r41 excerpt directly identifies the heard sound as the registry bell; unchanged JSON remains a weaker/possibly compatible encoding, so call it a loss of stated epistemic strength or fidelity, **not proven logical contradiction**; neither excerpt authenticates objective ringing. The novelty claim is falsified or unproven if reviewers reasonably treat the record as a compatible paraphrase with no material calibration distinction, or if they infer objective ringing from the stronger first-person claim. Keep all five JSON tables fixed and confirm only the targeted excerpt clause changes; checker exit status is irrelevant to this semantic judgment.
