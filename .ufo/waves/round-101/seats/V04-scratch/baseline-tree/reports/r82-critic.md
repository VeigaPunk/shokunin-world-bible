# Round 82 critic — Bell-7 evidence table

## Scope and comparator

I read the Round 82 declaration in `.shokunin/lore/LOG.md`, the available Round 82 date-scout report, the r35 Bell-7 excerpts and clause/pointer map, r35 critic and adjudicator, the r36 excerpts/date-contrast map and reviewer/adjudicator, and the r74 slate decision table with its critic and final adjudicator. At review time, `r82-date-scout.md` is the only delivered r82 report visible; this critique evaluates its proposed requests, not a later integrated artifact or an independent editor trial.

The source-text comparator is concrete. R35's fictional Tarin account names a sole seventh-watch Bell-7 on Embermonth 7, year 12; Sella's separate fictional account says “that same Bell-7” and names that same date. Their reported locations conflict only conditionally, with identity/place/occasion alignments assumed. R36 changes Sella's stated date to Embermonth 8 while Tarin remains on the seventh; the inherited JSON and ledger still say seventh. R36 reviewers correctly withhold a shared-occasion inference: the deictic “that same” and equal tick 3 do not cure the explicit date mismatch or stale structured rationale.

## Falsification: novelty and actionability

The proposed table is careful, but its *format* and much of its content closely reproduce r74's slate table: unresolved question/conditional possibility → specific hypothetical evidence → discriminating result → limitations, with repeated cautions that evidence may not exist and cannot establish truth or canon. Reusing that format is not itself a defect. The depth claim survives only if Bell-7-specific rows change what an editor would seek, beyond r35's already explicit occasion/identity/place cautions and r36's date-mismatch test.

There are plausible distinctions, but they are not all distinct actions as currently written:

- The first row (date/occasion records keyed to each particular ringing) and second row (a bell/watch schedule or complete event register to test whether Bell-7 is unique) overlap substantially. Both send the editor to a bell/watch register and require its scope, provenance, and linkage. The difference—linking these two reports to an occasion versus testing the “sole” premise—could matter, but the proposed examples do not reliably separate those requests. A schedule establishing a convention or possible uniqueness is not evidence that either note refers to its entry; a per-note entry could instead repeat the notes without being independent.
- The identity/place row is a distinct uncertainty, but r35's critic and manual map already name the person/place alignments as assumptions and distinguish Tarin's Harbor observation scene from Sella's reported Tower location. Asking for identity/location records may restate that caveat unless it identifies what precise unsupported mapping the editor would test and what source wording those records would be linked to.
- The provenance/capture row is a useful general safeguard, but it is generic source-independence advice also present in r74's limitations. “Original/capture provenance and custody” cannot by itself show independence; copied records can have traceable custody, and nominally separate records can share a source.

An editor could choose different questions (occasion-to-note linkage versus completeness/definition; then identity/place mapping), so this is not a proof that the table is useless. But the report supplies no blinded editor choice showing a materially different next step from r35/r36/r74. It also does not pin down an evidence request that could discriminate the r35/r36 date mismatch without simply asking for more records about the same Bell-7. Thus the declared before/after criterion is not yet met. **Provisional depth verdict: NO-GO / unproved**, unless an integrated table narrows overlapping rows and a non-author editor demonstrates a concrete changed evidence-seeking decision. Do not count row count or the r74-style table structure as depth.

## Calibration test: occasion linkage and evidentiary limits

The date scout generally keeps good boundaries: it calls proposed records hypothetical, distinguishes source claims from authenticated chronology, says equal tick 3 cannot identify an occasion, flags the stale r36 JSON, and explains the Tarin observation-scene/Sella-location pointer asymmetry. It explicitly separates fictional authored materials from real-source authenticity, fictional canon/truth, rights/permissions/training eligibility, and mechanics. These are appropriate and no rights or canon inference follows from the table.

Still, some “discriminating outcome” phrasing risks making the target premise sound more settled than the evidence could make it. “Records independently tying both accounts to the same unique ringing on one date” already builds in the very linkage at issue. The request is conditional and the limitation says a matching date alone is insufficient, but a reader could mistake a common label/date, matching entry, or register's own “sole” wording for a uniquely anchored same occasion. To earn that result, evidence would need independently supported linkage from *each account* to the same identifiable occurrence, with the record's scope/completeness and independence assessed; matching claims or a self-referential ledger is not that linkage. Likewise, evidence of “exactly one defined” ringing only tests what a record says within its scope; it does not authenticate occurrence or writer reference. The report's final caution substantially mitigates this risk, but not every row's result language carries it locally.

The conditional request for identity records also needs a strict boundary: independent linkage of two names to one person and place labels to locations would test editorial alignment, not show Tarin actually saw Sella or place Sella at either location. The report does state these limits. Attribution pointers/citations establish where this repository's authored fictional claims are recorded, not authenticity, truth, independence, or rights.

**Provisional calibration verdict: UNCHANGED, with a specific wording risk to check in any final table.** The report mostly calibrates correctly and does not assert that requested records exist, but a potential evidence result must not silently convert source-claimed Bell-7 into an established shared occasion. Calibration is not improved merely because caveats are numerous; it would be harmed if the final wording treats matching Bell-7/date or a structurally valid source pointer as independent confirmation.

## Falsifiable review test

Give a non-author only the exact proposed table and r35/r36 source excerpts/maps. Ask them, without coaching, to name the next concrete evidence they would seek for (a) whether the accounts refer to the same uniquely identified ringing, (b) whether “sole seventh-watch” is supported, and (c) person/place alignment—and to say what a positive result still would not prove. Compare those choices to what r35's existing critic/map and r36 date-contrast already direct an editor to do, and to the r74 template. Reject depth if the answers collapse to “find a reliable register / verify identity and provenance” without distinct discriminating requests. Reject calibration if the reviewer treats a shared label/date, repeated testimony, copied record, tick, or checker-clean pointer as proof of same occasion or actual location. A defensible positive answer remains conditional: repository excerpts are authored fictional reports, not authenticated sources; no canon, rights, or mechanics conclusion follows.
