# Round 76 critic — proposed fictional registry entry

## Scope and evidence

I read the r76 declaration in `.shokunin/lore/LOG.md`, the r73 Jorin slate excerpt, clause map, source row and claim, r74 critic note, and the r72/r75 critic precedents. The declaration proposes a separately attributed fictional registry entry reporting that Jorin was sent to copy a gate notice. It does not provide that candidate entry or its exact excerpt/map, so this is a declaration-level critique, not a review of a delivered artifact. I made no corpus or LOG edits.

## Strongest NO-GO: attribution is not corroboration, and the purpose is already stated

The r73 Jorin excerpt already says, “I was sent to copy a gate notice.” Its claim paraphrases that assignment, and its clause map points to the same words. A newly invented registry entry repeating this fact would not add a new perspective on Jorin’s purpose: that purpose is already present as Jorin’s own reported account. Assigning the repetition to a registry writer, ledger, or “record” may make it sound institutionally authoritative, but a source title or source ID cannot establish the writer’s access, provenance, independence, or truth.

The distinction is essential: the registry entry would be **newly invented fictional attribution**, not authenticated independent corroboration. Even if two fictional documents have separate IDs or named attributions, the fixture supplies no evidence that one is an independently preserved record, that its writer had independent knowledge, or that it corroborates Jorin. A new registry form can therefore manufacture the appearance of corroboration while merely echoing a known assertion. “Separately attributed” describes presentation; it does not prove an independent source relationship. No source truth, canon, rights, or mechanics follow from it.

The declared possibility is also weak as a novel editorial question. The known issue is already bounded in r73: Jorin reports being sent to copy a notice, but the sender and any link to another account are unspecified. Repeating the assignment from an invented registry perspective does not, without independently grounded detail, help discriminate who sent him, whether a request was made, whether he received or complied with it, or whether the slate was that notice. Any apparent resolution would come from invented authority rather than evidence. The r74 note treats future evidence requests as hypothetical, not proof that an archive or record exists; it does not license inventing a record and treating its label as provenance.

## Calibration and precedent

The r73 source and claim already carefully preserve attribution and uncertainty. A newly authored entry can add prose, a source row, and a claim structurally, but the checker cannot validate provenance, corroboration, entailment, or whether a source label has earned authority. As the r72/r73 critic precedents emphasize, source citations establish fixture attribution, not truth, and structured cleanliness cannot settle semantic support. The r75 duplicate-row precedent is also relevant: detectable structural distinction (including separate IDs) does not establish a material semantic distinction or an authoritative policy. Here, separate fictional records do not establish independent testimony.

Potential calibration harm is concrete if the entry is described as corroborating, archival, authoritative, a verified tasking, or independent without a stated fictional limitation. Even cautious wording such as “registry entry” may invite that inference unless the artifact makes clear it is newly authored, unverified fiction and the comparison explicitly denies any authentication or proven independence. Merely disclaiming truth does not itself create useful depth; it only avoids an overclaim.

## Verdict and limits

**NO-GO on the proposed depth gain.** Jorin’s reported copying purpose is already explicit in r73. A newly invented registry attribution that repeats it supplies neither a new Jorin perspective nor authenticated independent corroboration. The proposed move risks implying authority and laundering a known claim through a second fictional source. The r76 declaration itself identifies that no current second source corroborates the purpose; the proposed invention cannot fill that evidentiary gap.

This is a critique of the declared hypothesis, not proof that no future genuinely distinct fictional perspective could be written. A candidate would need a materially different, clearly bounded editorial function—not just another wrapper for the same assignment—and must label invented attribution without implying provenance or independence. The declaration’s limits on sender, receipt, actual sender, compliance, object identity, and time are appropriate but do not rescue redundancy. I did not inspect or validate a candidate, run a checker, or assess regression replay. No conclusion about source truth, canon, rights, or mechanics.
