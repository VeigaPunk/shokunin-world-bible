# Round 72 prior scout — entity-only Vela report

## Scope and declared test

This is a report-only prior-art comparison. The Round 72 declaration in `.shokunin/lore/LOG.md` proposes an r43-derived five-table fixture with a new fictional first-person Vela close-reading / tentative-maker-inference account, its own in-world source, and **one cited Vela entity claim only**—no timeline event, dependency, relation, or contradiction. It asks whether this yields meaningful depth without an unsupported shared tick. I did not build or run a candidate.

## R43 baseline: a cross-account place link

The r43 fixture contains Vela’s first-person report that she stood on the Lantern Harbor east steps, heard two strokes she identifies as the registry bell during her stated Bell-7/date, and did not see Sella or an envelope (`corpus/fixtures/r43-jorin-vela-sighting/vela-note-excerpt.md`). Jorin’s separate report says he saw Vela on those steps during what he called Bell-7, while not seeing who, if anyone, struck a bell (`jorin-note-excerpt.md`). Their clause maps explicitly limit this to attributed accounts: matching Bell-7 labels and tick 3 do not establish the same occasion, chronology, or objective ringing (`vela-clause-map.md`, `jorin-clause-map.md`).

The fixture represents those accounts with separate cited character claims (`entities.json`, `vela` and `jorin` rows) and separate timeline events, both at tick 3 (`timeline.json`, `vela-listening-report` and `jorin-vela-sighting`). R43’s non-author reviewer and adjudicator admitted a narrow depth gain because the new report specifically links to Vela’s already-reported location—not merely another unrelated witness or another row. That link remains conditional on shared place/occasion, and is not external corroboration or proof of a common Bell-7 (`reports/r43-reviewer.md`; `reports/r43-axis-adjudicator.md`).

## R71: distinct detail, but timeline order harmed calibration

R71 tested a different information unit: a newly authored Vela first-person account reports leaning close enough to read “KEEP THE EAST GATE CLEAR,” tentatively infers haste from cramped lettering, and says she neither saw nor knows the writer (`reports/r71-executor.md`; `reports/r71-reviewer.md`). The inherited r43 excerpts do not report that reading or maker inference. The non-author reviewer and Sol judged this a **narrow depth improvement**: it distinguishes a reported sensory observation from a fallible interpretation, rather than adding only rows or restating Vela’s hearing/location (`reports/r71-reviewer.md`; `reports/r71-axis-adjudicator.md`). It does not establish a slate, wording, author, or scene as fact.

The tested r71 candidate also appended a timeline event at `tick: 4`, while inherited report events occupy tick 3. Although the excerpt did not place the reading after those reports, the shared ordered timeline encoded that succession. The reviewer and adjudicator therefore found calibration harmed; a map disclaimer could not erase the ordering, and tick 3 or an earlier tick would imply other unsupported ordering/simultaneity (`reports/r71-reviewer.md`; `reports/r71-axis-adjudicator.md`). Sol rejected the trade: depth improved narrowly, coverage was unchanged, calibration was harmed. The candidate and README change were subsequently removed; R71 is precedent for the failure mode, not an active fixture.

## What entity-only changes—and does not establish

The declared R72 form is materially different from R71’s failed representation: a character claim can preserve a source-attributed report without placing it in the fixture’s shared ordered timeline. The active checker requires entity claims to have nonempty `statement` and `source_id`, then checks source-ID resolution (`corpus/fixtures/check_bible.py`, entity-claim validation); it does not read Markdown excerpts or clause maps, compare prose to claim meaning, or infer whether a claim is a dated event. Thus removing the timeline event can avoid the specific tick-4 ordering assertion, but it also means the structured representation offers **no timeline-event pointer** for the reported reading. The prose/map and the one cited claim must carry the attribution and clause trace; a clean checker result alone cannot establish that correspondence or depth.

The novelty is not simply “another Vela note,” a second source ID, or an added entity row. R40/R41/R43 already provide Vela’s auditory/location/non-sighting perspective and Jorin’s conditional sighting link (`reports/r71-prior-scout.md`; r43 excerpts/maps above). The candidate-specific novelty is the *reported close reading of literal slate wording plus an explicitly tentative inference about its writer*, absent from those inspected excerpts. Unlike R43’s cross-account location comparison, it would be a new within-report distinction between what Vela says she read and what she guesses. That is plausibly distinct and modestly meaningful, but not automatically depth: if the slogan and hurried-writer inference are merely decorative and do not make Vela’s partial perspective more inspectable, depth is unchanged. R71 reviewers’ narrow depth GO is relevant evidence for that prose distinction; it does not pre-judge a new entity-only candidate or its exact mapping.

## Falsifiers, limits, and scout judgment

For the declared candidate, compare the literal new excerpt with the single Vela claim and exact map. A non-author should be able to trace the quoted readable wording and tentative maker inference to that cited claim/source, while keeping the whole account attributed to Vela. Reject or call depth unchanged if the text repeats only the east-steps/Bell-7 account, the inference becomes a fact about writer or haste, the map/pointer is inexact, or claim-only representation proves inadequate to preserve the report/inference distinction. Confirm separately that no timeline event/order or hidden chronology is introduced, that the r43 inherited rows are unchanged except the declared source/claim append, and that the old 130 raw checker tuples are preserved. Regression and checker cleanliness are structural/preservation evidence, not narrative merit.

**Scout judgment:** R43’s narrow gain was a conditional cross-account location link; R71’s depth gain was a new reading-versus-inference perspective, offset by a harmful tick-4 succession encoded in its timeline event. R72’s entity-claim-only form is novel relative to that failed event-bearing implementation because it avoids assigning the report a shared tick, but gives up an event pointer and still needs exact manual prose-to-claim review. Depth is plausible, not established before implementation/review; coverage gain is not claimed; calibration risk appears reduced only if the entity-only wording/map do not imply timing or authentication. No source-truth, canon, rights, or game-mechanics conclusion follows from this comparison.
