## Scene-presentation test

The exact spans are: “I laid my map between us with a marked crossing toward the door” and “Mara turned it around and tapped a different crossing.” The second sentence supports framing two crossing options in sequence: the narrator’s marked crossing, followed by another crossing Mara taps. Removing it leaves only the first marked crossing and changes the literal presentation from one shown option to two. I therefore agree with the blind reader that this is a distinct, text-supported *framing* difference; the response is not absent or merely an editor’s invented gloss.

However, the candidate does not show a material, distinct useful scene presentation beyond restating that authored second beat. The clause map’s proposed alternative (“Two visible alternatives can be presented”) says no more than the excerpt itself: a different crossing is tapped. No consequence, stake, decision, changed understanding, obstacle, or tradeoff follows. The action establishes that the account presents another option, but not what distinguishes the options or why this particular framing earns material depth rather than simply including the newly authored sentence. The blind reader’s initial language about “disagreement” and a changed editorial choice should be narrowed: the excerpt does not establish disagreement, and its only counterfactual result is whether the second authored beat appears. On this record, that is a presentation difference, not a demonstrated useful consequence. This is the r79 issue in a two-beat form: an authored contrast is then cited as its own warrant. It is more concrete than generic characterization, but the candidate does not cross the declared materiality threshold.
