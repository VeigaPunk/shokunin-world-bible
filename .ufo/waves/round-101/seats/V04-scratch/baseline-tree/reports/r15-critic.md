## Falsification / calibration concerns

1. **Blue-mark inference:** “blue oval” next to the existing “Blue Seal” is a strong suggestive association even if the note disclaims identification. Readers may infer the mark is the seal’s trace, especially if the prose introduces no alternative context. This is a real ambiguity/association, not proof the stated uncertainty is false. Do not describe the mark as seal-made, as evidence of Mira’s route, or as a clue with a known cause. Avoid title/metadata/adjacent prose that silently converts resemblance into attribution.
2. **Source circularity:** If the executor writes both the excerpt and the timeline assertion from the same invented scene, matching wording is internal consistency, not independent corroboration. The `registrar-copy` source’s invented/non-omniscient limitation is appropriate; preserve it. The narrow defensible claim is that the fictional account adds a new reported observation, not that a separate source verifies the mark or its origin. Do not count citation identity or checker acceptance as prose-truth validation.
3. **Invented motive:** Existing text already attributes the blank and carbon retention to Mira’s request, but says nothing about her reason. The added account must not imply Sella saved the carbon to investigate, conceal, protect, or pursue the mark; none is established. “Looks again” supports a later inspection, but not why she did it. Keep the narrator’s uncertainty explicit and avoid omniscient motive language.
4. **First bell versus tick:** `registry-entry` currently uses tick 1, but its prose only says “first bell.” The source does not establish that first bell equals the model’s tick 1, that one tick is one bell, or elapsed time between bells. Keep the follow-up later than the entry through an explicit dependency/order if needed; do not infer a numeric conversion or state a bell count/date absent a source basis. Existing same-tick `registry-entry`/`departure` also means the data model does not resolve their within-tick order.
