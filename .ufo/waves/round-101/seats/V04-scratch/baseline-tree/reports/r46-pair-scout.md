## Novelty challenge and risks

This is **not novel merely because it concerns a person rather than a bell**. R41 already admitted calibration for a first-person source that directly identifies the bell while fixed JSON remains a weaker “took to be” rendition; it explicitly recognizes compatible-but-under-specific records (`.shokunin/lore/LOG.md:679-697`, especially 684, 696-697). R34 tests a shift from “saw” to “heard that,” where unchanged direct-observation claims are unsupported as direct sight (`.shokunin/lore/LOG.md:531-545`; `.shokunin/lore/reports/r34-pair-scout.md`, “Pair design”). R46 reverses neither lesson: it tests a *tentative identity of the observed person*, not sensory modality or bell/sound-source confidence. Nonetheless a reviewer may reasonably find this a thin instance of the same broad under-specific/epistemic-strength pattern. r43 itself already tests attributed identity/place alignment (`.shokunin/lore/reports/r43-reviewer.md`, “Clause-to-record trace”); that weakens novelty further.

Keep these bounds explicit: no objective identity evidence, no claim the person was not Vela, no authenticated Jorin or Vela identity, no implication of same occasion/shared Bell-7, and no update to Vela’s own reported location. A shared tick or map/ID cannot identify the sighted person. The JSON is not necessarily false; it is a categorical paraphrase that the changed excerpt no longer directly supports. Neither the prose nor citation proves canon, source authenticity, rights or permission. A checker-clean result would still be structural only.

## Falsifiable go/no-go

**Go** to the one-clause clone only if a non-author reviewer, shown the exact pair without a desired verdict, independently finds all three: (1) the new wording reports a sighting but leaves the seen person’s identity tentative; (2) fixed “seeing Vela” JSON may be true yet overstates what this excerpt directly identifies; and (3) this distinction yields material calibration beyond r41’s already-admitted compatible-but-under-specific confidence contrast and r34’s modality boundary. Reviewer must not infer non-Vela, error, or shared occasion. If (3) fails, **no-go**: prior art absorbs the distinction and no fixture is warranted. If tested, preserve all JSON tables byte-for-byte and change only that excerpt clause plus a new editorial map; checker output is expected unchanged, not evidence of semantic support. Any JSON alteration, direct-identification conclusion, falsehood/non-Vela assertion, stale map left unflagged, or novelty judgment reducible to “another confidence mismatch” falsifies the proposed gain.
