# Round 82 decision scout — prospective Bell-7 evidence requests

## Scope and anchors

This is an editorial proposal for what an editor could request *if* they wanted to test the assumptions behind the r35 paired reports. It does not assert that any proposed record, witness, or evidence exists. The excerpts and possible records below are in-world fiction; references to the repository identify the existing fictional text, not authenticated sources outside it.

Exact clauses that frame the decisions:

- Tarin: “I, Tarin, saw Sella place an unlabeled paper envelope on the Lantern Harbor registry desk during Bell-7, the sole seventh-watch registry-bell sounding on the seventh day of Embermonth, year 12.” (`.shokunin/lore/corpus/fixtures/r35-bell7-conflict/tarin-note-excerpt.md:1`)
- Sella: “I, Sella, was physically at North Tower throughout that same Bell-7 ringing and was not at Lantern Harbor at any point during it. Bell-7 is the sole seventh-watch registry-bell sounding on the seventh day of Embermonth, year 12.” (`.../sella-bell7-note-excerpt.md:1`)
- Both excerpts explicitly caution that Bell-7/date are claims in the fictional notes, not authenticated chronology, clock time, or timeline tick (`.../tarin-note-excerpt.md:3`; `.../sella-bell7-note-excerpt.md:3`). R35's map says the fixture-local names and place IDs are editorial alignments, Tarin's `harbor` place is his reported observation scene, and tick 3 is not occasion evidence (`.../manual-clause-pointer-map.md:5-17`).
- R36's controlled near-miss changes Sella's stated date to the eighth day while Tarin remains on the seventh, and leaves the JSON event and ledger rationale at the seventh; its report says the “same Bell-7” label and tick cannot bridge the date mismatch (`.shokunin/lore/reports/r36-pair-scout.md`, “Controlled substitution”; `.shokunin/lore/reports/r36-date-scout.md`, “Risks”). This is a calibration example, not evidence that either fictional report is false.

|---|---|---|---|
| **Occasion: do both reports identify one uniquely anchored ringing/date, rather than reuse a label?** | If the editor needs occasion alignment, request a provenance-described, date-bearing in-world bell register or other contemporaneous record that independently identifies the particular seventh-watch ringing, plus a traceable link from each note to that entry. | **Identity: is the person Tarin says he saw the same individual as the first-person “I, Sella”?** | If identity is the uncertainty, request a provenance-preserving account from an independent observer who could identify the person at the claimed placement, or contemporaneous identifying details linked to the occasion and to Sella by a separately attributable identification record. Seek identification, not merely another use of the name “Sella.” | A well-linked identification matching the observed person to the person who authored the Sella account would support the fixture-local identity alignment. | **Place/scene: does Tarin's Harbor clause identify where the reported placement occurred, and are Harbor and North Tower distinct places for this comparison?** | If place alignment is the uncertainty, request a source tied to the described registry desk that independently identifies its physical location, and a separately attributable place plan or record locating North Tower relative to that desk. Keep the requested identification of Tarin's observation scene separate from evidence of Sella's own whereabouts. | Confirmation that the desk is at Lantern Harbor and that the named Tower is a distinct location would support the place-ID distinction used by the pair. In particular, Tarin's `place_id=harbor` records the scene of his reported observation; it is not by itself a claim that Sella was there. |
| **Source independence (optional, orthogonal): are apparent corroborating records independent of the two accounts?** | Only if an editor proposes using a record as corroboration, request its provenance and transmission history, including whether it was copied from Tarin's or Sella's account. | A traceable origin independent of both reports would make it a potentially distinct source for the specific detail it records; dependence on either account means it cannot serve as independent corroboration of that detail. | Independence does not make its contents accurate, and does not settle identity, occasion, place, or canon by itself. No such record is alleged here. |

These are different decisions, not variants of one generic “find more sources” request: the occasion request tests temporal/occasion linkage; identity tests who Tarin's reported “Sella” is; place tests what physical scene the place wording denotes. They may be considered separately and none presumes the others. Even favorable results would support only the corresponding alignment, not the whole conditional conflict automatically.

## Precedent and limits

R35's ledger condition is expressly conditional on the names and place IDs aligning and both reports concerning the sole Bell-7 on the seventh day; it preserves both accounts unresolved and says the checker validates pointers, not semantic incompatibility (`.shokunin/lore/corpus/fixtures/r35-bell7-conflict/contradictions.json:9`; `.shokunin/lore/reports/r35-ledger-scout.md`, “Risks”). R36 demonstrates that identical Bell-7 wording alongside a changed stated date does not independently establish one occasion. Neither equal local ticks nor structurally valid citation pointers resolve those questions.

R74's slate table offers conditional tests for different surfaces, one surface read at different unknown times, and inaccurate wording, but those are slate-specific possibilities (`.shokunin/lore/corpus/knowledge/r74-slate-wording-decision-table.md`). This proposal does not transplant those slate hypotheses to Bell-7: it asks about a reported occasion, a reported person's identity, and the observation/place distinction peculiar to the r35 clauses.

All proposed records and requests are hypothetical in-world editorial devices, not claims of actual source authenticity, source existence, canon, rights or permission, or real-world or game mechanics. No real person, location, document, or historical fact is being authenticated. A checker pass would establish only the structure it checks; it would not answer any request above or decide which report is true.
