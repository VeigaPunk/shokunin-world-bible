# Round 76 independent reviewer — fictional registry-entry hypothesis

## Scope and decision

**Provisional NO-GO** on the declared depth move. I reviewed the active r76 declaration and scout synthesis in `.shokunin/lore/LOG.md`, all eight r76 scout reports, the delivered r73 Jorin excerpt/claim and its reviewer/adjudicator, the r74 slate-wording decision table, and the withdrawn-r75 incident boundary. No r76 fixture candidate exists. This is an independent review of the hypothesis, not a candidate or regression verification; I made no corpus, checker, or LOG edits.

The proposed minimal entry—“Copy request entered for Jorin: gate notice.”—does not add a materially different, actionable editorial perspective. It restates the same copying purpose Jorin already reports: “I was sent to copy a gate notice.” Changing the grammatical frame from Jorin’s first-person account (“I was sent”) to a registry-style statement (“request entered”) offers a conceivable distinction between a reported request and a reported sending, but supplies no basis to decide whether either is a distinct event, who recorded it, or whether it is connected to Jorin’s account. As proposed, the distinction is tautological in editorial use: the only useful question it raises is the same unresolved question of who requested/sent Jorin, while the entry provides no grounded answer.

## What the prior material already does

R73 attributes the assignment, slate reading, and limit on who Jorin saw to one newly authored, fictional, unverified report. The excerpt and claim do not name a sender, request wording, receipt, compliance, notice identity, or time. The map preserves those limits. R73’s admitted depth was the attributed contrast between Jorin’s reported “OPEN” wording and Vela’s “CLEAR,” with their object, time, and source relationship unresolved; its reviewer/adjudicator did not treat a second source ID as corroboration.

R74 already turns those unresolved issues into editorially actionable evidence questions: distinguish surfaces; link observations to one identified surface and establish ordering; or assess a report against a capture tied to its particular occasion. The table explicitly marks these as conditional evidence to seek, not records that exist. A newly invented copy-request entry does not answer or sharpen those tests. The r76 scouts correctly distinguish attribution from provenance: a new fictional narrator/source ID can say something different in form, but cannot authenticate a registry, establish independent origin, or corroborate Jorin merely by sounding institutional.

The withdrawn concurrent r75 depth declaration is incident telemetry only: it was withdrawn and unexecuted, with no scout dispatch, candidate, or evidence. I do not treat it as a prior completed round, support for r76, or evidence about the fictional events. Its overlap is relevant only as proposal-history context; this NO-GO rests on delivered r73/r74 material and the r76 hypothesis itself.

## Could an added detail be justified without borrowed authority?

Not on the evidence reviewed. A detail such as a named requester, exact notice, request wording, date, status, or registry procedure could create a genuinely different question, but each would be newly invented rather than derived from Jorin’s excerpt or r74. It could still be written as openly authored fiction, but the registry label would not justify it, and it would not be an evidence-backed depth gain for this proposed move. The least-specific version avoids some invented particulars only by repeating what is already known from Jorin’s report.

A future fictional perspective need not claim real or in-world documentary authority to be useful; it would need a clearly distinct narrative/editorial function that is not just a second wrapper for the same assignment, and reviewers would need to find that function materially useful. No such function is identified here. A disclaimer that an entry is invented and unverified prevents an authenticity overclaim; it does not itself make the entry novel or actionable. Do not describe it as independent corroboration, an authenticated registry record, proof of actual tasking, or evidence of receipt/compliance.
