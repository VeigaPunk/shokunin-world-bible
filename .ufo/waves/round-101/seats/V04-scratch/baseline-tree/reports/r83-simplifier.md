Both encode separate non-sightings in attributed language; neither says Sella or an envelope was objectively absent.
- On the ordinary coordinated reading, “did not see Sella or an envelope” denies seeing either one. “Did not see Sella with an envelope” denies seeing Sella accompanied by an envelope; it does not say whether Vela saw an envelope alone. Thus the fixed claim/event would overstate the changed excerpt if read as reporting envelope-alone non-sighting. A suitable editorial repair is to narrow the claim/event paraphrase to the joint non-sighting, or withhold any separate envelope-absence report. The changed prose does not assert that Vela saw an envelope.
- Prior r30 explicitly contrasts “did not carry” with unsupported proximity and bounds the negation’s temporal reach (`reports/r30-negation-scout.md`; `reports/r30-axis-adjudicator.md`). That is predicate negation versus positive/negative evidence, not coordinated-object versus joint-phrase scope. The r28–r32 work examined carrying, explicit negation, and source-row screening; I found no exact Vela coordinated-negation-scope contrast. R34 tests direct seeing versus “heard that,” and r36 tests date mismatch; they are not scope tests.

If they cannot distinguish the permitted first repair (separate envelope non-sighting) from the joint-only wording, or judge the difference immaterial/hair-splitting, reject the candidate.
