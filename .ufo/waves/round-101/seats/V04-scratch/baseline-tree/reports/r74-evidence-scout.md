## Questions an editor could investigate (not claims that evidence exists)

| Live possibility | Discriminating in-world evidence to seek, if available | What it could distinguish; residual limits |
|---|---|---|
| **Separate surfaces**: the accounts may concern different slates/inscriptions. | A contemporaneous, independently attributable record for each reading that identifies the physical surface (for example, a distinctive mark or inventory identifier) and its location, with enough provenance to link each account to that surface. Seek evidence of *two distinct identified objects*, rather than relying only on different locations or the witnesses’ wording. | Distinct surface identifiers tied to each reading would support the separate-surface explanation over a single-object account. A location alone, matching text, or a later recollection would not establish object identity. Even reliable proof of two surfaces would not show either witness’s wording accurate or inaccurate. | **One surface, readings at unknown times**: the wording may have differed across readings, but the accounts supply no reading dates or sequence. | First seek independent identification tying both reports to the *same particular surface*. Then seek time-anchored, provenance-preserving records of that surface’s inscription at each reading (such as independently dated direct copies or images), plus a supported ordering of those observations. | Same-object linkage plus records showing different text at ordered times would distinguish a possible change from merely different surfaces or an unlocated reading. A single snapshot, an undated copy, or the fixture’s shared path/place wording cannot show when a difference arose. Even ordered records would not by themselves prove who changed anything, when between observations it happened, or why. The current sources provide no timestamps or common-object identification. |
| **Inaccurate testimony / transcription**: at least one reported wording might not match what was on the relevant surface at that person’s reading. | Seek an independent, contemporaneous transcription or image demonstrably tied to the same identified surface and reading occasion, preferably with an independently documented chain from capture to record; compare it with each witness’s contemporaneous account, if one exists. | This could test accuracy of a particular reported wording without presuming which witness is wrong. It would not establish intentional deception: error, memory, copying, or later transcription could remain alternatives. Without common surface and occasion linkage, a different text in another record cannot identify an inaccurate witness. The excerpts do not establish any corroborating capture or transcript. |

These are evidence requests, not additions to the fictional record. Keep each candidate evidence item conditional (“if available”); do not write as though a duplicate slate, dated image, registry record, or independent witness is already present. A later source would itself need attribution, provenance, and a stated limit; a citation or structurally valid fixture pointer alone does not authenticate it. Even if a proposed discriminator were found, record what it supports and what remains unresolved rather than selecting canon by default.

## Editorial caution

The r72/r73 notes establish only that Vela and Jorin are separately attributed as reporting different wording. Neither excerpt gives a reading date; Vela does not name a location in the slate-reading excerpt, while Jorin places his reported reading at the east steps. This difference does not identify separate surfaces, and the shared word “slate” does not identify one surface. Neither excerpt proves objective wording, a change, a common occasion, or a false report. This scout proposes no source of truth, canon, rights, or mechanics inference.
