The strongest GO case, from the simplifier, is a narrowly scoped mismatch finding *after* a unique event and its source have resolved: this could draw attention to an apparent duplicate pointer without rejecting arbitrary unknown keys or selecting another citation. That is technically feasible, and `source_id` is more suggestive than a generic extra key. But the matched controls demonstrate only that the checker ignores it. Neither they nor the independent reviewer show a human using the copied value as citation, changing an attribution, or making a distinct repair because of CLEAN. Under the current documented convention, the immediate correction for an unwanted copy is to remove that ledger-side member; it is **not** evidence for changing the target event's citation. A warning that makes this newly enforced constraint without demonstrated review benefit would turn tolerated metadata into a policy exception. A blanket unknown-key ban or independent resolution of the copied ID would be still less warranted.

Round 63 is an analogy, not a ruling that all extra members are safe: an ignored extension can be accepted without a material change in a consumed conclusion, and new strictness needs its own consequential use and compatibility case. Here the suggestive citation-like name makes confusion plausible, but not established. Existing r7/r39 event-citation diagnostics concern the real target event's source, not this extra side key. The non-author reviewer reaches the same NO-GO on attribution/repair materiality; I agree on the evidence rather than treating that review as a substitute for a demonstrated reader decision.
