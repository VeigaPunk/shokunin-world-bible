## Proposed fiction (not corpus text or established lore)

> I wrote the names in the order people reached my desk, not the order the clerk called them. If I copied the clerk’s order, the people who had waited longest would look as though they came last.

This is newly invented, unverified first-person prose. ## Specific editorial action

**Keep the narrator’s arrival-order list as the scene’s visible reading order, rather than silently rearranging it to match the clerk’s call order.** That choice puts the waiting people—not the clerk’s procedure—first in the reader’s view, and makes the narrator’s stated reason for changing the order legible. It is a concrete staging/ordering decision supported by the invented speaker’s explicit choice and rationale, not a generic trait such as being observant or persistent. It survives removing any job title or authority label: neither appears in the excerpt. The excerpt does not require a named place, date, event, or connection to another account.

The useful question for a non-author reviewer is whether preserving that order would make the scene’s point clearer, or whether the excerpt is still too slight to earn a distinct editorial choice. Reject the depth claim if the answer is merely “the narrator is considerate,” with no meaningful reason to retain that ordering on the page.
