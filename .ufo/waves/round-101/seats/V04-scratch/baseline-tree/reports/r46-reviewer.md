# Round 46 non-author lore review — tentative person identification

## Independent decision

**NO-GO for a new calibration admission on the current evidence.** The proposed wording presents a genuinely useful distinction about *which person* Jorin reports seeing, but it does not establish a material new calibration decision beyond the confidence/identification contrast already admitted in r41. The difference in referent (a person rather than a bell) and reversed direction (direct-to-tentative rather than tentative-to-direct) are not, by themselves, a new decision rule. No candidate fixture was executed or created for this review.

## Evidence and bounded reading

R43’s excerpt says “I saw Vela standing on the Lantern Harbor east steps”; its Jorin claim and timeline event say Jorin reports seeing Vela. These are attributed statements in the authored, unverified fictional source, not objective verification. In the proposed variant, “I saw someone I took to be Vela,” Jorin still reports seeing someone at the steps but marks the identification as tentative. This excerpt does not directly identify that person as Vela.

The unchanged “Jorin reports seeing Vela” claim/event remain logically compatible: the person may have been Vela. They are more definite than this altered excerpt directly supports; that is a source-to-record precision/support limitation, not proof the JSON is false, contradicted, or that Jorin was mistaken. The proposed text does not prove the person was non-Vela, nor supply any alternative identity. Citation/source IDs do not resolve the identity.

The implication for the cross-account link is conditional and narrow. Jorin’s account would no longer directly identify the person at the steps as Vela, so it weakens the basis for linking Jorin’s sighting to Vela’s *reported* location. It does not alter Vela’s own reported position. Even under the original direct-identification wording, the link depended on the accounts referring to the same place and occasion; “what I called Bell-7,” matching labels, or local ticks do not prove a shared occasion. Neither version establishes a common Bell-7 event, a bell sounding, canon, or source authenticity.

## Novelty and falsifier

R34 distinguishes direct sight from hearing an assertion; it is not the closest precedent. R41 is: it already required distinguishing tentative from direct source identification while fixed JSON remained logically compatible but less faithful in epistemic strength. R46’s person-identity proposition can sharpen the *application*—identity of an observed referent gates the conditional cross-account link—but the proposed outcome is still the same core calibration: a tentative source identification does not warrant the record’s unqualified identification, without making that compatible record false. Reversing direction or changing “bell” to “person” alone does not establish an incremental calibration gain.

**Falsifier for this NO-GO:** a blind, independent comparison would need to demonstrate a distinct decision consequence not reducible to r41’s strength-fidelity judgment—for example, a separately specified person-resolution rule that changes what may be linked across accounts while preserving uncertainty, and that reviewers apply reliably without inferring a different person. If such a result is shown, reconsider narrowly. If reviewers merely say “compatible, but the source is more tentative than the record,” the r41 precedent absorbs the case and no new gain should be claimed.

No checker result, fixture, or candidate experiment is claimed here. Depth and coverage are not implicated; calibration is **unchanged** for this proposed new move. No conclusion about objective identity, non-Vela identity, shared occasion, or canon follows.
