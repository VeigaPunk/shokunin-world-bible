## Duplication and attribution risks

The candidate’s likely added proposition—“a request was directed to Jorin”—is nearly the same semantic content as Jorin’s r73 “I was sent to copy a gate notice.” A fictional registry writer can be *attributed* as making that statement. Merely authoring a second invented document, assigning it a separate source ID, and citing it does not make it independent in provenance, authenticated, or corroborative. It can at most create another authored fictional report. If its content is copied or paraphrased from Jorin, the source count and distinct document form cannot establish a second information origin.

There is a possible editorial distinction between Jorin’s first-person claim that he “was sent” and a registry entry that *reports a request*. But a request record alone cannot establish who sent him, that he received or complied with it, that the record is contemporaneous, or that it refers to the slate/reading in r73. If the entry merely restates the request, its distinct question is weak and duplicates the existing clause; if it claims independent record provenance, that authority is invented unless supported by evidence outside this fictional authorship exercise. The declaration itself recognizes that limitation. Do not convert proposed fictional attribution into “independent corroboration.”

R72/R73/R74 already have separately cited fictional source rows and clause maps; R73’s copying assignment is already represented in the same Jorin claim as his slate reading. Adding another source/claim may improve pointer granularity but is not, by itself, a new editorial test or material depth. No new timeline, relations, or contradiction entries would be appropriate: the existing reports do not establish the registry request’s time or connection to the reading, much less a shared occasion or artifact.
