## What citation/checking means

`check_bible.py` checks required table/row structure and that claim `source_id` resolves to exactly one source row (unknown and duplicate IDs are diagnosed). It does not read Markdown excerpts or maps, compare quotation text, establish semantic entailment, authenticate a speaker/document/inscription, or adjudicate whether two reports refer to one object. A clean result would establish structural citation-ID resolution only. Text-to-claim support and exact clause-map fidelity require a human comparison against the excerpt; a citation pointer is not a truth certificate.

The README explicitly distinguishes `source_id` / `first_appearance` as references to **in-world fictional records** from the separate real-world inspiration reference ledger. An invented source ID therefore denotes fixture attribution, not external research, corroboration, or evidence of authenticity. It cannot establish source-text truth, slate existence or actual inscription, writer/placer identity, artifact identity or persistence, reading time, chronology, canon, or game mechanics. It also says nothing about copyright, license, reuse permission, or training rights; this report makes no rights finding.
