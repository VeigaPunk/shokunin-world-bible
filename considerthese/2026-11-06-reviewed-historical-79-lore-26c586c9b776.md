# Lore: imagined addressees and actual recipients

[Sterne, Tristram Shandy](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/39270/39270-h/39270-h.htm), Book I, chapter XX: the narrator stages a reader's objection and rebuke. This is composed address, not evidence of an actual response or endorsement of gendered judgments. Gutenberg39270 transcribes the1912 Everyman edition with later reprint imprints; layout can differ from lifetime editions.

[Schmid, Narratee](https://www-archiv.fdm.uni-hamburg.de/lhn/node/60.html), revised October2013: projected addressee and factual recipient serve different functions; imagined rejoinders differ from autonomous dialogue. This argued distinction offers an analytical lens, not consensus or a direct study of Sterne.

For a world bible, a narrated complaint does not automatically authorize adding an independent cast member. Reception remains an authorial decision rather than an inferred reader reaction. Selected passage and analytical sections only; no adaptation tested.
