# Lore: an edition includes its surrounding invitations

[Genette](https://assets.cambridge.org/97805214/13503/excerpt/9780521413503_excerpt.pdf), French1987, English1997, describes accompanying material that presents a literary work. Location, timing and audience matter; readers need not encounter that material in the assumed order. The retained frontmatter identifies digital printing in2001.

[Pignagnoli](https://cf.humanities.uva.nl/narratology/issue/7/pdf/102-119_Pignagnoli.pdf) extends this framework to material presentation and digital supplements. Her proposed functions overlap rather than form rigid categories. This is an interpretive framework, not measured audience reception.

The earlier distinctions still help compare editions. Digital supplements add another place where narrative context and invitations can change. A companion's existence alone does not establish that a booklet is self-contained or that readers will visit it.
