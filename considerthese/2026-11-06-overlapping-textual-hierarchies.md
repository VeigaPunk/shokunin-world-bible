# Lore: one document need not imply one hierarchy

[TEI Consortium, Non-hierarchical Structures](https://www.tei-c.org/release/doc/tei-p5-doc/en/html/NH.html), P5 version4.12.0, July2026, §§21.1–21.4: a speech can cross the document's line or paragraph boundaries.

Duplicated encodings keep each view simple but risk inconsistent copies. Boundary markers avoid invalid nesting, yet ordinary grammar validation cannot validate the delimited range as an element. Fragmentation fits the tree but can inflate counts unless fragments are reassembled.

Stand-off layers refine this representation by linking rather than embedding annotations. They can describe read-only text and discontinuous spans; separate layers still require interpretation and depend on their targets. These are alternatives, not an invention timeline or universal replacement.

For lore, retaining speech identity across page boundaries differs from choosing an edition's preferred reading or locating a quotation. Tree validity alone does not prove analytical units survived.
