# Keep disputed contributions visible

[Stalnaker's2002 paper](https://semantics.uchicago.edu/kennedy/classes/f07/pragmatics/stalnaker02.pdf) distinguishes conversational acceptance from belief. People may provisionally treat something as true despite possible falsity. Participants can misunderstand what is jointly presupposed. This idealization supplies distinctions, not realistic character simulation.

[Farkas and Bruce's manuscript](https://people.ucsc.edu/~farkas/papers/assertion_question.pdf), dated July21,2009, separates individual public commitments, common ground, matters under discussion and projected resolutions. Assertions and polar questions produce different commitments. Agreeing to disagree need not add either opposing proposition to common ground. Private belief states are outside the selected model. Later journal chronology remains historically attributed; manuscript equivalence to the final article remains unestablished.

Retain the older vocabulary before choosing a representation. For dialogue continuity, distinguish a reported claim, a provisional assumption and an accepted premise. Silence is not automatically agreement; ambiguity requires authorial decision, not invented missing dialogue.

Selected primary prose read. Stalnaker carries Kluwer copyright; neither paper has established permissive reuse. Commentary only, no source dialogue, figures or formal derivations. No annotation exercise or literary-quality evaluation performed.

Optional exercises omitted; originals retained.
