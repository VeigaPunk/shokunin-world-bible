## Depth claim is not established

Vela’s existing first-person account places her on Lantern Harbor’s east steps, says she heard the registry bell strike twice during her attributed Bell-7, and says she did not see Sella or an envelope (`.shokunin/lore/corpus/fixtures/r43-jorin-vela-sighting/vela-note-excerpt.md`). Its map explicitly aligns the steps only to broad `harbor`; it does not locate her at a slate, establish sightlines or legibility, or prove objective bell/date facts (`.../vela-clause-map.md`, clauses for east steps, Bell-7 and non-sighting). The new premise that she “could read” wording on an east-step registry slate thus adds an unestablished observation opportunity: nothing cited shows the slate existed, was at that location, faced her, was readable from her reported position, or was read during the claimed occasion. A first-person fictional sentence can invent these events, but source citation and clause map cannot make that invention a plausible witnessed event within the existing fixture. Unless the excerpt itself supplies a bounded, credible basis for seeing and reading it, the proposed epistemic distinction (“read” wording versus inference about maker) is a disclaimer around a new unsupported observation, not demonstrated depth.

The addition is also at risk of being redundant. Vela already provides her own location/sensory perspective; Jorin already reports seeing Vela on the east steps, while his clause map carefully limits this to a report-level, conditional location overlap (`.../jorin-note-excerpt.md`; `.../jorin-clause-map.md`). As the r43 critic and reviewer note, that comparison did not establish a shared Bell-7 occasion or objective event (`.shokunin/lore/reports/r43-critic.md`, “Falsification risks”/“Verdict”; `.shokunin/lore/reports/r43-reviewer.md`, “Precedents and depth judgment”). A new slate statement could add a distinct reported object only if its exact wording and credible witness circumstances make it narratively informative. Merely appending a further Vela claim/event row, or saying she read text and guessed at its author, is not by itself a material distinction from the existing attributed reports. No exact proposed sentence is supplied to show such a distinction.

## Chronology / inference traps

The inherited Vela report’s Bell-7 and year/date are source claims, not authenticated chronology; the r43 maps say matching Bell-7 language and tick 3 do not establish a shared occasion or simultaneity (`.../vela-clause-map.md`; `.shokunin/lore/reports/r43-axis-adjudicator.md`, “Clause, precedent and falsification check”). The r43 fixture also already contains incompatible Tarin/Sella reports tied to their own claimed Bell-7; its manual map explicitly treats the occasion/date as fictional claims, and tick 3 as arbitrary local sequence (`.shokunin/lore/corpus/fixtures/r43-jorin-vela-sighting/manual-clause-pointer-map.md`). Therefore “contemporaneous” must not slide from a same-time *observation* into a claim the slate was written, posted, or read during the same Bell-7, nor from the same `tick: 3` into corroborated timing. “What I could read” does not alone establish when wording was written or who wrote it. A maker inference must remain Vela’s inference, not a hidden authorship attribution. The declaration’s stated limit against claiming actual authorship, seal provenance, or authenticated time is necessary but not sufficient without the literal text and field values to inspect.

The checker cannot close these gaps. `check_bible.py` validates JSON shape, IDs/citations, relation and timeline input constraints; it does not read prose excerpts or clause maps, test whether a witness could see a slate, compare wording to source support, authenticate dates, or establish chronology. A clean run would be structural evidence only.

## Verdict and falsifier

**Depth: no-go / unproven on the declaration provided.** It is plausible to author a new fictional Vela statement, but the extant sources/maps give no basis for her opportunity to read the alleged slate, and there is no exact excerpt with which to test redundancy, mapping, or chronology leakage. Do not represent the report as a recovered/contemporaneous record or as plausible witnessing merely because it is first person and cited. Coverage and calibration are not claimed improved; no conclusion about fictional-world truth, canon, rights, or mechanics follows.

A candidate could falsify this critique only by showing the exact excerpt and maps, with a bounded witnessed vantage that makes the slate and its wording plausibly legible, every clause matched to its cited source/claim/event, and clear separation of text actually read from Vela’s inference about its maker. It must not imply when the words were written, a shared Bell-7/time, or chronology through a copied tick. A non-author should then explain what meaningful perspective this adds beyond Vela’s existing east-steps/bell account and Jorin’s conditional sighting, rather than counting rows. The current declaration alone cannot answer those checks.
