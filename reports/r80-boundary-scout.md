# Round 80 boundary scout — attribution and inference limits

## Scope

Report-only boundary review of the Round 80 declaration in `.shokunin/lore/LOG.md`, the Round 80 action/alternative/prior scout proposals, and the relevant Round 77, Round 79, and Round 73 reports and r73 fixture. No candidate fixture is reviewed here; the proposed vignettes are not corpus text or admitted lore. I made no corpus, LOG, checker, or other report edits. No agents were spawned.

## What is and is not in-world attribution

Round 80 requires a new, explicitly fictional, cited account with a deliberate action and a separately described observable response/consequence. Its action and response are still newly authored content. A source row can label that account fictional and unverified; an entity claim can say that the named speaker *reports* those clauses. This is attribution within the fixture, not evidence that the event happened or that the response is an independently authenticated fact.

The existing r73 fixture is a useful caution. Vela’s separately attributed report says “KEEP THE EAST GATE CLEAR”; Jorin’s says “KEEP THE EAST GATE OPEN.” Their comparison is limited to two reported wordings. R73 does not establish one slate, inscription, event, or timeline, and the two wording reports do not corroborate a new Round 80 vignette. The inherited Jorin Bell-7 sighting/tick concerns his report of seeing Vela at the east steps; it does not date either slate reading or any new action. A fixture clone carries those rows for preservation, not as evidence or context that connects added prose to the slate.

## Source IDs, structure, and prose support

If a vignette is implemented, give its new fictional account a distinct `sources.json` ID and cite the added claim to that ID. Do not recycle `jorin-slate-note`, `vela-slate-note`, or inherited character/source rows to make a new speaker or action look sourced. A new ID plus a claim/source pointer establishes a fixture-local reference only. The checker validates schema and citation-ID resolution; it does not read excerpts or clause maps, check that a claim is entailed by the exact words, establish causal coherence, or decide whether a response provides a materially useful scene alternative. A clause map is a human pointer aid, not another witness. Human review must compare each claim and purported consequence with the exact excerpt clauses.

In particular, the alternative scout’s map-rotation example contains a second character, Mara, and two crossings; those are hypothetical invented particulars, not canon geography, an established relationship, or an institution. The cup/mark action scout is likewise only a proposal. If either is used, keep claim language attributed, do not silently turn scene staging into a durable relation or world rule, and judge whether the exact response supports the stated alternative rather than relying on the scout’s gloss. A source ID does not resolve that materiality test.

## Overreach risks to keep out

- **Institution/title:** Do not give the actor institutional authority, a gatekeeper/clerk/official role, or an established procedure unless the task separately supports it. Even words such as “sent,” “assigned,” “desk,” or “clerk” can imply an organization or process; if present as invented first-person wording, attribute them narrowly and do not promote them into a world-bible institution or policy.
- **Timeline/event:** Do not add a date, watch, tick, dependency, or shared occasion merely to make the vignette legible. Inherited r73 tick-3/Bell-7 rows are not a timestamp for the new account. A two-beat order inside an excerpt supports only that the speaker narrates action then response; it does not link the vignette to an older event or establish a broader chronology.
- **Relations:** Do not encode a durable relation between the new actor and Mara, Jorin, Vela, a clerk, or an institution just because they share a passage or a cloned fixture. Do not infer collaboration, authority, witness, or source dependence without direct support.
- **Slate/gate linkage:** Do not call a new mark, map, crossing, gate, or object the east gate/slate from r73; do not use “OPEN” or “CLEAR” to explain a physical state, motive, or consequence. The r73 reports leave slate identity, occasion, timing, and source relationship unresolved. Mere shared fixture or nearby gate vocabulary supplies no link.
- **World rule / consequence:** A fictional response can be a scene consequence (for example, a visible blur or changed map orientation) without proving a general rule, real institution, canonical geography, or objective outcome. Do not upgrade a possible editorial reading into those claims.

These are boundaries, not findings that any proposal asserts the overreach. The declared test requires a non-author to identify two genuinely different scene presentations from exact clauses and a consequence beyond repeating the action or the speaker’s rationale. Whether that succeeds is for actual-candidate review; this boundary scan does not award a depth gain.

## Separate conclusions

- **Invented in-world attribution:** A properly labeled new source and claim can attribute an invented account to a speaker; they do not authenticate it.
- **Citation-ID/structure checks:** Unique, resolving IDs and clean schema checks establish pointer/structure integrity only. They do not establish text entailment, truth, or editorial value.
- **Prose truth, canon, rights, mechanics:** Neither fictional attribution, checker output, nor scene-review verdict establishes source authenticity, fictional-world truth/canon, real-world rights or permissions, or game mechanics. This report makes no finding on any of those matters.
