# Round 46 precedent scout — person-identification uncertainty

## Assessment

**Conditional go for a narrow manual calibration probe; novelty is real but modest and contested.** The proposed change is not merely the r41 bell-confidence contrast repeated with a new noun: it changes the referent-level assertion from direct identification of a person to an explicitly tentative identity attribution. The r43 report says “I saw Vela standing…” (`.shokunin/lore/corpus/fixtures/r43-jorin-vela-sighting/jorin-note-excerpt.md:1`), while its claim/event retain that Jorin reports seeing Vela (`.../entities.json:87-95`; `.../timeline.json:50-56`). The proposed “someone I took to be Vela” no longer directly identifies the observed person as Vela. A reviewer should therefore withhold *direct identification of the person as Vela from this excerpt*, not infer the observed person was someone else.

This is a person-referent issue, not uncertainty about what object made a sound. Still, both r41 and the proposal concern source-to-record fidelity under a change in epistemic strength, so the general technique and calibration target substantially overlap. Reversing the direction (r41 tentative→direct; this proposal direct→tentative) alone is not a new capability. The defensible incremental value is whether reviewers track **which proposition is uncertain**: identity of a seen person here, versus identity of an auditory source in r41. If the proposed test merely asks reviewers to notice a weaker phrase beside a stronger fixed JSON statement, it duplicates r41 and should be no-go.

## Precedent comparison

- **R34 modality:** r34 contrasts Tarin “saw” with “heard that” (`.shokunin/lore/corpus/fixtures/r34-tarin-hearsay/tarin-note-excerpt.md:1`; `.shokunin/lore/reports/r34-pair-scout.md:5-11`). The unchanged `reports seeing` rows are unsupported *as direct-sighting evidence* by the hearsay excerpt (`.../entities.json:51-71`; `.../timeline.json:23-29`; `.shokunin/lore/reports/r34-reviewer.md:3-8`). R46 retains visual seeing; it does not substitute hearsay or change the observation modality. It narrows the identity of the seen referent. R34 remains relevant because both demand separating evidence support from falsity, but it is not the same distinction.
- **R41 confidence:** r41 changes Vela’s “two strokes I took to be the registry bell” to “heard the registry bell strike twice,” while fixed claim/event retain the tentative identification (`r40-vela-listener/vela-note-excerpt.md:1`; `r41-vela-direct-bell/vela-note-excerpt.md:1`; `r41-vela-direct-bell/entities.json:81`; `r41-vela-direct-bell/timeline.json:46`). Its map explicitly says the fixed wording is logically compatible but under-specific, not false (`r41-vela-direct-bell/strength-contrast-map.md:3-5`), and the adjudicator admitted calibration on this narrow fidelity distinction (`.shokunin/lore/reports/r41-axis-adjudicator.md`, “Substantive calibration test” and verdict). That is a serious novelty challenge: r46’s unchanged direct-identity row beside newly tentative prose is the reverse mismatch, and may be judged as the same strength-fidelity skill. Do not claim novelty from direction alone.
- **R43 direct identification:** Jorin’s authored excerpt explicitly says “I saw Vela”; the Jorin claim and event encode Jorin reporting that same identification (`r43-jorin-vela-sighting/jorin-note-excerpt.md:1`; `entities.json:93-95`; `timeline.json:55-56`). The source row describes an unverified fictional first-person report (`sources.json:32-36`). The proposed clause weakens only the person referent in Jorin’s account; Vela’s own report that she stood at the steps remains untouched (`vela-note-excerpt.md:1`). This changes the conditional cross-account location link: it no longer directly supports that Jorin saw Vela there, but it does not unsettle Vela’s own reported position.

## Risks and scope

The unchanged JSON remains logically compatible with a tentative account if Jorin’s observation could in fact have been Vela; it is stronger and less faithful to the changed excerpt, not thereby false. “Someone I took to be Vela” does not objectively establish Vela’s presence, establish a non-Vela person, or negate Jorin’s visual observation. The record/source mismatch is about what this excerpt supports as an identification, not the truth of the fixed row.

Likewise, separate Jorin and Vela texts do not authenticate either identity, prove independent provenance, or show a shared occasion. “During what I called Bell-7” is Jorin’s label; the proposal supplies no objective time, common occasion, bell event, non-Vela identity, or canon. The checker’s structural CLEAN result cannot adjudicate prose entailment. Do not edit the inherited r43 maps/rows or represent the proposed excerpt as observed evidence.

## Falsifiable go/no-go

**Go only as a bounded manual contrast** if a non-author, shown the exact r43 excerpt/claim/event and the one-clause tentative-identity variant with records held fixed, independently states all of the following: (1) the variant reports seeing someone but does not directly identify that person as Vela; (2) it therefore withholds this excerpt’s direct support for the Jorin→Vela location link, without concluding the person was not Vela or that the JSON is false; (3) Vela’s own reported location is unaffected; and (4) no shared Bell-7 occasion or canon follows. The reviewer must explain why this referent-specific judgment adds a useful discrimination beyond r41’s admitted tentativeness/directness strength-fidelity test, rather than merely reciting the same generic mismatch rule.

**No-go** if the reviewer accepts the tentative phrase as direct identification, infers a different person/objective contradiction, treats the JSON as disproved, or cannot articulate an incremental distinction from r41. On current precedent the experiment is marginal: direction and person-referent make it distinguishable, but novelty is not established until that falsifiable review succeeds. No fixture/checker/README/LOG change is warranted by this scout alone.
