# Round 79 critic — depth-claim falsification

## Scope and evidence

## Strongest falsifier: a citation cannot turn invented prose into editorial evidence

The declaration’s before→after test is a useful hurdle, but it does not yet identify an editorial decision whose answer actually depends on the candidate. R72 and r73 already provide separately attributed fictional readings with materially different wording (“CLEAR” / “OPEN”), while the r73 comparison leaves object, time, and source relationship open. R74 already gives an editor concrete, conditional next-evidence choices for that discrepancy: distinguish separate surfaces, investigate one identified surface with ordered observations, or assess a particular report against an occasion-linked capture. A new first-person source that merely adds a detail, motive, physical action, or character impression does not change those choices. The r77 latch attempt is the close precedent: novelty of subject and an attentive-sounding action were not enough to establish a distinct useful question beyond decorative texture.

There is a more basic limit. These are explicitly invented, unverified accounts. A new fictional narrator can be a legitimate *authorial choice* in a story, and the editor may decide to include or omit that invented scene. But its source ID, claim pointer, and exact clause map only locate and attribute the authored prose inside the fixture. They do not independently warrant a setting fact, establish character truth, or provide evidence that an editorial action is required. If the proposed decision is “show this fictional character doing X,” that decision risks being circular: the editor invents X in the source and then cites that invention as the reason to depict X. It can still be an intentional creative choice, but that is not demonstrated evidentiary depth. The candidate needs to name a concrete alternative (what the editor would do without the passage versus with it), why the passage supports choosing one, and why this is not just a preference supplied by the author/editor.

The declaration itself requires the decision’s warrant to survive stripping title/authority, unrelated r72/r73 linkage, and editor speculation. That is exactly where a claim built from an invented standalone vignette is vulnerable: remove its framing and the slate linkage, and a generic action or sensory detail may remain, but no distinct decision follows from it. Conversely, any value that depends on a familiar institution, a “witness” role, a common occasion, or a connection to the existing slate reports imports unsupported authority or relation. The declaration’s own prohibitions correctly rule those out; the remaining materiality burden is still unmet until a specific candidate demonstrates otherwise.

## Falsification tests for any candidate

1. **Name the changed decision.** State one observable editorial choice with a real before/after difference, not merely “include this detail,” “show personality,” or “ask another question.” Identify the competing choices and what the excerpt—not an invented backstory or editor gloss—discriminates between them.
2. **Strip the props.** Remove title and institutional/occupational authority; remove all r72/r73 comparison and slate references; remove the editorial question’s interpretation. If no distinct choice remains in the literal excerpt and bounded claim, the depth gain is decorative or speculative.
3. **Test necessity and non-circularity.** Ask whether the editor could make the same choice from existing r72/r73/r74 evidence, or whether the new sentence only repeats/relabels an established uncertainty. Ask whether the supposed warrant is merely the author’s new invention being used to justify that same invention. If so, no material depth is shown.
4. **Check attribution boundaries.** Compare every mapped excerpt span with the claim and source pointers. Preserve “reports,” uncertainty, and limits; do not convert an invented observation into fact, corroboration, canon, chronology, or a relation to Vela/Jorin/their reports. A defensible fictional creative choice is not independent-world evidence.

## What the checker can and cannot establish

`check_bible.py` checks JSON shape, required nonempty claim statements/source IDs, and whether IDs resolve uniquely; it performs structural checks on relations, timeline, contradictions, and optional item provenance. It does not open prose excerpts, clause maps, or comparison notes; verify quotation-to-claim entailment; assess narrative usefulness; authenticate a source; or determine world truth. Thus `CLEAN` would mean the citation identifier resolves under the checker’s schema, not that the cited source text supports the claim, that the source is true, or that the fiction is canon. Even exact clause mapping is a human editorial audit, not source authentication.

Nor do these materials establish real-world rights, license, ownership, or permission; a fictional-source label and a clean checker are not rights findings. They establish no game mechanics. Keep those separate from the fictional editorial judgment.

## Verdict and reversal condition

**Provisional NO-GO on declared depth; no candidate-specific verdict is possible yet.** The hypothesis identifies a standard for a possible gain but supplies neither a distinct decision nor evidence that a candidate can meet it. Existing r72/r73/r74 material already supports the known slate-wording editorial questions, and r77 shows how a novel, concrete detail can remain generic texture. Depth is not improved merely because a new fictional claim is cited or because an editor can imagine a use for it. Coverage is unchanged by the declared source/entity claim shape, absent a new query or retrieval capability. Calibration is not improved by disclaimers alone; no measured harm is established because no r79 candidate has been delivered.

Reverse this NO-GO only if a non-author reviewer, given the literal candidate and the stated alternatives without its title/authority, slate linkage, or interpretive gloss, can independently identify the same specific, useful, observable decision and explain how the excerpt changes that decision beyond existing r72/r73/r74 evidence. Reject if the reviewer can name only mood, generic characterization, texture, speculative backstory, or a decision whose warrant is the invented sentence itself. No inference here about source-text truth, canon, rights, or mechanics.
