# Round 56 precedent scout — holder-reference ambiguity

## Independent verdict: NO-GO (material calibration not established)

The proposed diagnostic is implementable and the checker currently silently suppresses chain validation for duplicate holder IDs. But the precedent set argues against treating a second local message as a material calibration gain: round 6 explicitly required duplicate holders not drive chain judgments, and round 11 explicitly left provenance-holder ambiguity outside its narrowly justified place-reference rule. The global duplicate-ID finding identifies the duplicated entity rows; a reader can already see the referenced holder ID and the chain is withheld. Adding one field-local message may improve navigation, but that is not yet evidence of a better calibrated conclusion. This scout recommends stop before fixture/checker edits absent evidence that the global finding is insufficient for users to locate the affected provenance assertion.

## Materiality and falsifier

The proposed output could make an exact field easier to locate, but the extant global duplicate diagnostic plus the raw record already exposes the same identifier; the checker withholds the chain judgment rather than issuing a misleading chain-break verdict. That is a plausible no-material-gain case. Do not claim round 11 proves this move impossible: its scope was different. Rather, its explicit exclusion means novelty is unproven and requires fresh evidence.

Falsifier for this NO-GO: a non-author operator/reviewer demonstrates, on an admitted duplicate-holder input, that the global duplicate finding and preserved raw record do not let them identify which provenance reference caused chain suppression, while a field-local `from_holder`/`to_holder` diagnostic does so without suggesting which entity is intended or implying a chain break. If that shows a material calibration improvement, reconsider with a paired unique-holder control and separate one-field tests for `to_holder` and non-null `from_holder`; keep the global duplicate finding, raw rows, and existing chain suppression unchanged. Count zero must retain dangling behavior; count one must retain present outputs; exclude malformed rows from cardinality. Exact pre-existing raw-output delta accounting would then be required.

## Collateral and hard boundaries

Potential collateral is noisy duplicate reporting (one global duplicate plus one or more per-reference messages), apparent multiplicity of evidence, and readers mistaking a local diagnostic for an assertion that there are multiple real holders or a broken/false custody history. A duplicated ID may be repeated at several provenance positions; output count should be per affected field, but this repetition itself may add little information. The proposed checker diagnostic would describe only ambiguity among structurally admitted IDs in this input. It cannot establish actual custody, source support, object identity, chronology outside the local listed-chain convention, canon, rights, or truth. Keep global duplicate reporting and all raw provenance regardless of any future decision; do not resolve the holder.
