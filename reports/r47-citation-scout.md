## What an intake record can and cannot establish

If written exactly as proposed, the new text can establish only that this authored fixture depicts an intake record whose wording reports (a) an unlabeled paper envelope at the desk, (b) a Bell-7 occasion as named by the record’s writer, and (c) no depositor entry in that record. “Depositor not recorded” is a statement about the record’s contents or recording practice. It does not show that no depositor existed, that no one placed the envelope, that Sella did or did not place it, or that Tarin’s account is accurate.

A matching `item` identifier and object-presence claim/event could make an explicit editorial comparison with Tarin’s reported envelope-at-desk observation. It cannot identify the two objects as one envelope, establish a shared occasion from the repeated Bell-7 label or equal local tick, or prove the actor/placement. The phrase “the envelope” risks falsely implying identity; use “an unlabeled envelope” and describe the comparison as a possible parallel, not a match. An envelope is not thereby a depositor, observer, or mover. If the schema requires an event actor, the source must expressly support that actor performing the observation/recording action; do not assign the envelope as actor or invent an observer role merely to satisfy the schema.

The slip and excerpt would be newly authored fiction. A unique source ID is only a local pointer; checker CLEAN is structural and does not establish that prose entails fields, that a record existed in-world, independent corroboration, truth, canon, ownership, licensing, or permission to reuse/train on text. Do not make rights claims from the fictional source label.
