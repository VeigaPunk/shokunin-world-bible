# Round 6 scribe — bounded item-provenance decision table

## Observed repository state

- Round 6's declared claim in `.shokunin/lore/LOG.md` proposes a cited item-provenance rule: an explicit transfer from A to B at tick T must match the immediately prior holder recorded by an acquisition or earlier transfer; mismatches should be local chain breaks. It also requires absence of provenance to remain distinct from conflict and unrelated bibles not to fail for omitting transfer data.
- The current `.shokunin/lore/corpus/fixtures/check_bible.py` requires five arrays (`sources`, `entities`, `relations`, `timeline`, `contradictions`). Its entity schema has `kind`, `first_appearance`, and `claims`; its relations have `from`, `to`, `type`, `source_id`; timeline records have `tick` and `source_id`. It validates source IDs and relation endpoints, but has no acquisition/transfer field or item-chain check.
- `.shokunin/lore/corpus/fixtures/consistent/entities.json` contains item `seal` with first appearance `courier-log` and a cited claim that it accompanied Mira. `consistent/relations.json` says Mira `carries` the seal, citing `courier-log`. Neither record specifies an ordered handoff or prior holder. These observations do not establish an item history.

## Proposed decision table (recommendation; not implemented or tested)

For this table, an optional explicit provenance record is either an **acquisition** (`item_id`, `holder_id`, `tick`, `source_id`) or a **transfer** (`item_id`, `from_holder`, `to_holder`, `tick`, `source_id`). The records are proposed structured assertions, not facts inferred from prose. Item and holder IDs must resolve; the item must have `kind: item`; each record needs a resolvable in-world source. Ticks use the existing nonnegative integer convention. An acquisition seeds the holder chain; later transfers continue it. Ordering/tie policy must be deterministic; same-item equal ticks should be treated as ambiguous input, not arbitrarily ordered.

| Input condition | Recommended interpretation / report | Evidence boundary |
|---|---|---|
| No provenance records for an item (including a bible with no optional provenance data) | No provenance-chain diagnostic. Treat history as **not represented**, not as a missing holder or a clean/complete history. | The current clean fixture has no transfer data; its `carries` relation and claim do not supply a prior-holder chain. This is the no-hard-fail boundary in the round-6 LOG claim. |
| One cited acquisition, followed by zero or more transfers whose `from_holder` equals the current recorded holder | Chain is structurally continuous; no chain-break diagnostic. Advance the recorded holder to each transfer's `to_holder`. | This verifies consistency of the represented records only; it does not verify that a cited source is truthful or that the chain is complete. |
| A transfer has no preceding acquisition or earlier transfer for that item | Report **insufficient represented predecessor** at that transfer; do not assume its `from_holder` is the true prior holder. | Enforces the LOG's “from a cited acquisition/previous transfer” condition without inferring an origin from `first_appearance`, `claims`, `carries`, timeline prose, or gameplay. |
| A transfer's `from_holder` differs from the holder established by the preceding acquisition/transfer | Report a **chain break** at that transfer, showing the expected and stated holder and record location; preserve all records. | This is the declared positive/negative rule. It is a structural inconsistency among assertions, not proof which assertion is canon. |
| Two cited records assert incompatible holder histories (for example, competing acquisitions or branches that cannot form one ordered chain) | Report the conflicting records/locations as a conflict or ambiguous chain; do not choose a source, repair the sequence, or label one record canon. | The existing checker describes itself as “report, never resolve”; `corpus/fixtures/contradictions/` retains conflicting assertions rather than selecting a winner. Exact conflict grouping remains a design choice, not implemented behavior. |
| Provenance row has invalid shape, unknown item/holder, non-item target, invalid tick, or unknown `source_id` | Report a row-local schema/reference/citation issue; do not use that row to establish a holder. | Analogous existing checks are in `check_bible.py` for required fields, dangling entity references, nonnegative timeline ticks, and source IDs. No provenance-row diagnostic has been measured. |
| Two records for one item have the same tick and ordering cannot be established | Report ambiguity (or require a documented explicit sequence field); do not invent ordering or evaluate a guessed chain. | Existing timeline ticks permit ordering checks but do not define same-tick order for proposed provenance records. This policy needs agreement before implementation. |
| Item is mentioned in an uncited claim or `carries` relation, with no explicit provenance record | No inferred acquisition or transfer; apply the ordinary existing citation checks to those rows only. | `consistent/entities.json` and `consistent/relations.json` show the concrete distinction: current citations attest rows structurally, but no ticked holder transition is encoded. |

## Scope and status

This table interprets the round-6 claim; it does not document observed checker output, provide fixture results, or claim the proposed rule is implemented. The schema and decision details above are recommendations. A later test would need committed raw cases for absent data, a seeded valid chain, a predecessor mismatch, and relevant malformed/ambiguous cases, plus non-author replay. No transfer is inferred, no history is repaired, and no source's truth or exhaustive provenance semantics is asserted. The existing clean/contradictory outcomes must remain unchanged if the proposal is implemented.
