# Round 46 critic — Jorin’s tentative person identification

## Finding

**Serious novelty challenge; calibration candidate is not novel on the evidence inspected.** The changed referent makes this a potentially sharper *instance*—who was seen, rather than what made a sound—but it still tests the same prose-to-fixed-record confidence/fidelity boundary already admitted in r41. Treating the direction of the hedge as novel is not enough: r41 already compared tentative→direct prose while retaining stronger/weaker fixed JSON and required a reviewer to recognize compatibility without asserting falsehood. R46 reverses that direction (direct→tentative prose, fixed record direct), but appears to ask the same editorial skill. Unless the test demonstrates a materially different decision beyond relabeling the referent and reversing polarity, recommend **no-go on a new calibration claim**.

It does not by itself settle R46; r41 is the stronger novelty challenge.

## Risks / limits

1. Do not call the fixed JSON false or strictly contradicted. Its direct identification may be compatible with Jorin having seen Vela; the source now provides only a tentative identification. Phrase the limitation as **this excerpt does not directly establish Jorin’s identification as Vela**, not “the person was not Vela” or “Vela was not there.”
2. Keep attribution: the excerpt is a fictional, unverified first-person report (`.../sources.json:31-35`), not authenticated evidence. Citation ID, clean checker output, or the existing `vela` entity ID cannot resolve a person’s identity.
3. The evidentiary consequence is narrow and conditional: r46 would weaken the source’s direct support for a Jorin-to-Vela location link. It does not unsettle Vela’s own reported position, prove independent non-Vela identity, synchronize Bell-7, or show that Jorin and Vela shared an occasion. R43’s map expressly limits place/occasion overlap (`jorin-clause-map.md:5-10`).
4. A contrast map can teach the distinction but cannot make this a new axis gain by itself. No blind/generalized reviewer calibration is shown in the cited r41 precedent; do not claim broad calibration improvement from one editorial judgment.

## Falsifiable go/no-go

**Current recommendation: NO-GO for novelty/admission absent a discriminating test.** If the parent proceeds to review, blind the reviewer to the expected verdict and ask separately: (a) what does r43 directly report about the identity of the person? (b) what does the changed excerpt support about identity, and what remains possible? (c) is unchanged “Jorin reports seeing Vela” false, contradicted, merely more specific than this excerpt, or directly supported? (d) what happens to the conditional cross-account location link? A calibrated answer should say the r43 excerpt directly attributes seeing Vela, while the altered excerpt reports seeing someone Jorin took to be Vela; it withholds direct identification support from that excerpt but does not establish non-Vela identity or make the fixed row false. It should say only the conditional Jorin-to-Vela link is weakened; Vela’s own reported location and common-occasion uncertainty remain.

For **go**, show that this identity-specific decision is materially distinct from r41’s tentative/direct attribution task—not merely “same confidence mismatch, opposite direction”—and that the reviewer distinguishes lack of direct identification from evidence of a different person. Otherwise **no-go**: retain the report as an attempted novelty challenge, make no calibration gain claim. Checker cleanliness would establish only JSON structural validity, not source-to-record fidelity or identity. No objective non-Vela, shared occasion, or canon conclusion is supported.
