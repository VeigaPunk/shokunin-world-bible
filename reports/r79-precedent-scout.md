# Round 79 precedent scout — bounded materiality precedent

## Scope

I read the Round 79 declaration in `.shokunin/lore/LOG.md`, the actual round 72, 73, and 77 reports and their relevant fixture material. This is a report-only precedent summary for the declared test: a fictional cited source and entity claim must support a *specific useful editorial character/setting decision*, not merely add novel or vivid prose. I did not edit LOG, corpus, checker, or other files; made no commit, push, or merge.

## Precedent

- **R72 — narrowly admitted depth against its stated r43 baseline, not new gain over r71.** The actual Vela excerpt distinguishes her reported reading of “KEEP THE EAST GATE CLEAR,” her tentative inference that cramped lettering might mean a hurried writer, and her inability to identify the writer. The reviewer found this a modest reading-versus-inference perspective, but explicitly noted substantial reuse of r71’s slate-reading/hurried-writer idea. The claim-only structure was adequate for an attributed report but intentionally lacked event-level queryability. No event, chronology, relationship, or corroboration was added. Thus r72 supports a *narrow* depth precedent where prose makes a bounded perspective/inference distinction legible; it does not establish a high bar of consequential editorial action or justify counting repeated material as incremental gain.
- **R73 — narrowly admitted depth from a useful, bounded contrast, with no coverage/calibration gain.** Jorin’s actual excerpt gives a reason to attend to the notice (“sent to copy a gate notice”), reports “KEEP THE EAST GATE OPEN,” and says he did not see who placed it. Compared with Vela’s separately reported “KEEP THE EAST GATE CLEAR,” the reviewer found a specific unresolved wording contrast and an editorial question, while expressly leaving common slate, occasion, time, and source relationship unestablished. The reviewer and final report call this a small narrative perspective gain, not corroboration, a verified inscription puzzle, or plot fact. Coverage and calibration remained unchanged. This precedent’s contribution is not just another source/citation: the distinct wording plus the in-excerpt copying purpose made a concrete comparison available without inventing a link.
- **R77 — rejected despite topical novelty, valid attribution structure, and checker cleanliness.** Neri’s actual unnamed-gate line (“Its latch caught only after I lifted it twice”) and editorial question suggested generic attentiveness/persistence but did not yield a specific useful characterization, setting distinction, or editorial choice after stripping role/slate assumptions. Non-author reviewer and final adjudicator rejected material depth. They treated proximity to r73 gate vocabulary as an association risk, not proof of shared gate/occasion or calibration harm. Exact claim/map pointers and clean checker output did not make decorative texture material. This is the closest negative precedent for r79’s explicit materiality test.

## Bounded bar for the r79 hypothesis

The precedent supports requiring the actual excerpt and attributed claim to let a non-author identify a distinct, concrete editorial choice or interpretation that is useful beyond “new detail,” generic personality, or decoration. Test the proposed use after removing title/authority, speculation, and any unproved r72/r73 slate linkage. The asserted use must survive as a decision grounded in the excerpt itself; if it depends on an invented event, timeline, relationship, causation, or a presumed common slate/occasion, the cited fiction does not supply that warrant. A useful question is not itself evidence of materiality: r77 had a legitimate editorial question and still failed. Conversely r72/r73 do not require plot revelations; their accepted gains were explicitly narrow, but r73’s comparison had a concrete, bounded editorial object.

This is precedent, not a verdict on a not-yet-evaluated r79 candidate. It does not require mechanics or rights analysis. The declaration’s baseline is the current r78 HEAD, and r72/r73/r77 decisions should not be mistaken for evidence that their fictional accounts are true or linked.

## Structural versus substantive evidence

In these rounds, `source_id` resolution, JSON pointers, literal excerpt spans, and `check_bible.py` CLEAN results establish limited structural facts: a cited ID resolves under checker rules, the data passes the implemented schema/relationship checks, and (where manually compared) a map reproduces text spans. They do **not** establish that the quoted fictional report is true, that its source is authentic or authoritative, that a slate/object/event existed, or that the prose semantically warrants an editor’s conclusion. A clause map is an editorial aid, not a checker-validated proof of entailment. Regression byte/CLI equality establishes preservation of tested baseline inputs and outputs, not truth, canon, rights, mechanics, or narrative value.

All described sources and excerpts are invented fictional material explicitly treated as unverified. No source-text truth, canon/chronology, rights, or game-mechanics finding follows from these precedents or from citation-ID checks.
