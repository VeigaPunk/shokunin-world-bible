## Axis judgment

- **Depth — unchanged.** No new fictional account or better-supported story claim was added. Four test fixtures and an ID guard do not deepen the lore.
- **Coverage — unchanged.** Count-zero place references now receive more carefully limited occupancy assertions, but dangling-place detection already existed; the round does not establish another contradiction class or general location handling. Previously tested cases retain their output. Duplicate/ambiguous place IDs and unique wrong-kind entities still enter raw-ID occupancy comparisons despite separate diagnostics, as the reviewer probed; this is an explicit residual, not a coverage claim.
- **Calibration — harmed overall in the submitted evidence, notwithstanding the checker-level improvement.** `r16-missing-place/timeline.json` gives its *dangling* `unlisted` row the assertion “A separate record places Mira at North Tower at tick 7.” `r16-missing-first/timeline.json` similarly says “A record places Mira at Lantern Harbor at tick 7” for `unlisted`; `r16-mixed-three` repeats the North Tower description for its dangling row. `tower` and `harbor` are present, named entity rows in these very fixtures. Their descriptions thus tell the human reader a known location and apparent same-tick harbor/tower clash while the machine reference intentionally says its location is unresolved. The source-ID field and uninspected source prose cannot repair that mismatch; nor may one silently map `unlisted` to the named place. The documentation says descriptions do not resolve IDs, correctly describing the checker, but it does not remove the contradictory human-facing claims inside the new fixtures. As demonstrations of *uncertain location*, they invite a false sense that the withheld booking is both known and unknown. This is a material calibration regression in the example artifacts even though the code itself is more cautious. Do not infer the checker reads descriptions, proves same-place, establishes no conflict, or resolves canon.

The criterion requires at least one axis improved and none harmed. A cleanly worded missing-location fixture could retain the independently observed checker gain, but the current artifacts fail that criterion. This verdict is confined to the submitted round, not a claim that the ID guard should never be used.
