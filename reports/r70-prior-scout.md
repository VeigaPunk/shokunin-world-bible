The R69 reviewer described a plausible repair use for that warning: independently correcting both invalid fields to the same allowed field would still leave identical references. The adjudicator judged depth unchanged, coverage unchanged in the no-change tree, and calibration improvement unproved; neither usefulness nor harmlessness of the R62 warning had been established. See `reports/r69-axis-adjudicator.md` (decision and axes), `reports/r69-reviewer.md` (R62 repair-risk rationale), and the R69 closure in `LOG.md`.

R70 does not simply repeat that rejected predicate: its declaration gates on nonempty-string `event_id` only, leaving field equality as the existing raw comparison. Thus an invalid field alone does not disable comparison when both event IDs are usable. This distinction addresses the specific R69 counterexample, but the R69 no-go is not a positive ruling on this narrower proposal, nor may R70 be treated as retroactively admitted in R69.

## Novelty, materiality, and limits

The raw same-reference concept, its unconditional implementation, and malformed-field behavior are not novel. The specific proposed eligibility condition—both sides must have nonempty string event IDs, while matching raw fields need not be allowed—is a narrower refinement not established by the cited R62/R69 decisions. R70 itself reports baseline probes where two empty sides receive required-ID errors plus a pair-level finding comparing absent values; R69 reports independently observed the same empty-both behavior. That supports a limited calibration concern: the pair finding supplies no usable event-ID target in that case. It does not alone demonstrate material user benefit from removing it. No repair study is reported, and no evidence here resolves whether other unusable-ID shapes have distinct repair value.

A meaningful assessment should keep the R62 shared-valid-ID/invalid-field control, valid identical and different controls, and missing/blank-ID cases distinct; verify side-local diagnostics remain; and compare full outcomes rather than count alone.
