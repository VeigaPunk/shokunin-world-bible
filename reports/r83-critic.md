# Round 83 critic — falsification of negation-scope gain

## Verdict

**Do not count a material calibration gain yet.** The proposed wording difference is linguistically plausible, but the declared before/after inference is overstated, and the evidence so far does not establish a materially different editorial decision beyond the already practiced manual prose-to-record mismatch review. At most this is a candidate narrow scope example; this critic report is not an admission or a recommendation to change the corpus.

## Falsification points

1. **The baseline does not separately encode two absence propositions.** In `corpus/fixtures/r40-vela-listener/entities.json`, `vela.claims[0].statement` says Vela “reports not seeing Sella or an envelope”; `timeline.json` repeats the same coordination in `vela-listening-report.description`. Neither row has separate Sella/envelope fields or separately sourced claims. The excerpt (`vela-note-excerpt.md`) is first-person fictional text, and the map treats the phrase as a report-level non-sighting, not proof either was absent. Ordinary English favors reading “not … Sella or an envelope” as neither target being seen, but that is a human reading of one coordinated sentence—not two independently represented facts. The declaration's phrase “stronger separate non-sighting of Sella and envelope” should therefore be qualified as a likely distributive interpretation, not claimed as encoded structure.

2. **“With” is a plausible contrast, not a fully determinate test oracle.** “I did not see Sella with an envelope” naturally denies seeing the joint Sella-plus-envelope scene. It does not entail that Vela saw either one separately, nor that she saw neither. But “with” can express association/context, and the excerpt specifies no interval, vantage, or whether “with” means jointly present/held/carried. A reader could reasonably take it as a narrower scene description without construing it as a formal predicate `see(Sella AND envelope)`. The contrast is useful only if the map states that intended joint-scene reading and a blinded reader actually distinguishes it from the original coordinated negation. The checker cannot adjudicate that interpretation.

3. **The advertised envelope-alone repair is not the exact record delta.** The old claim/event paraphrase says “not seeing Sella or an envelope”; it does not say “did not see the envelope alone,” and it is not an explicit pair of standalone absence assertions. A candidate with joint wording would make that broader paraphrase an over-reading under the ordinary interpretation; a natural repair is to narrow the claim/event to “did not see Sella together with an envelope,” or mark separate sightings unspecified. That is a real possible wording repair, but it is not evidence that the baseline encoded two separate fields, and not by itself a materially new editorial action. The semantics scout's suggested repair is prospective, not an observed non-author decision.

4. **Prior art is adjacent and the general operation repeats.** R28–r30 already train a reviewer to distinguish support, unsupported wording, and local negation against unchanged records, while rejecting global/canon conclusions. R34 changes fictional source modality with JSON fixed and asks a reader to qualify the mismatched direct-sighting records; r36 changes a date and asks a reader to withhold an inherited same-occasion inference. Those do not test coordination versus joint-scene scope, so the lexical distinction is not an exact duplicate. However, the proposed outcome—notice that a changed excerpt no longer supports the same free-text paraphrase, then narrow that paraphrase—is the same manual calibration pattern as r34/r36. A new semantic label is not automatically incremental calibration.

5. **Fixture targeting deserves care.** The round declaration names an r40 clone. The semantics scout instead discusses `.shokunin/lore/corpus/fixtures/r72-vela-slate-claim/` as its source. Both reportedly contain the quoted clause, but the candidate's control, exact claim/event, and citation map must be pinned to one fixture before any comparison; otherwise “same record, one prose delta” is not demonstrated. R40 scout reports that r41/r43 also retain this non-sighting wording, so this wording is not unique to the proposed control.

## What would falsify this NO-GO

A non-author comparison should see the exact r40 excerpt/map and fixed claim/event, then the exact one-clause joint wording and corresponding map, without being told the expected answer. Require the reader to state (a) what each clause licenses about Vela's *reported* sighting, (b) which specific retained claim/event phrase is too broad in the candidate, and (c) the minimal repair. The distinction counts only if the reader identifies the original as likely denying each target separately, the candidate as denying only the joint configuration, and chooses a narrower claim/event wording or explicitly leaves standalone sightings unresolved. If the reader gives the same permissible paraphrase/repair for both, treats “with” as equivalent, or cannot locate a concrete overstatement in the free-text records, the calibration-gain claim fails. A different explanation of the same generic “source changed, qualify record” action is not enough.

## Evidence boundaries

These excerpts, maps, and record descriptions are authored fictional fixture content attributed to `vela-note`; that attribution does **not** authenticate a source or establish Vela's identity or observation. The checker validates JSON shape and references, not Markdown wording, negation scope, or prose-to-record entailment; either variant being CLEAN would show structure only. This is no finding about what occurred in the fictional world, canon, actual presence/absence, a shared Bell-7 occasion, real-world rights, or game mechanics. No positive sighting or absence follows.
