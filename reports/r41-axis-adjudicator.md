## Substantive calibration test

R40 has Vela report *two strokes she took to be* the registry bell. R41 instead has Vela *directly identify* what she reports hearing as the registry bell striking twice. Both are first-person fictional claims. Neither supplies external verification of the bell's identity, sound, location or actual ringing. Fixed `entities.json` says “Vela reports hearing two strokes she took to be the registry bell”; fixed `timeline.json` likewise says she “reports hearing two strokes she took to be the registry bell.” Those entries do not assert objective bell truth. They are **logically compatible** with Vela's stronger r41 statement as weaker characterizations, so declaring them false, strictly contradicted or unsupported would fail calibration. Yet “took to be” imports a tentative identification not expressed by her direct wording. It loses the distinction between *her stated identification* and *the recorder's tentative rendition*: r41 is less precise source-to-record encoding than r40. This is a fidelity issue, not an entailment failure or world-fact verdict.

This is more than another generic prose edit. R34 requires withholding *direct-sighting support* when the excerpt changes from seeing to hearing an assertion; r36 requires withholding a *shared seventh-day occasion* when Sella's excerpt says eighth day. Their fixed records assert specifics no longer supported by the changed source. R41 reverses that direction: a **stronger attributed statement** confronts a **weaker, still compatible record**. A binary “matches/contradicts” review, or a generic observation that the words differ, misses this distinction. The controlled single-clause pair and non-author review expose the precise third answer—compatible but confidence-under-specific—while refusing to turn direct source identification into objective proof. This is a modest, material calibration gain for manual source-to-record interpretation; it is not evidence of blinded reviewer generalization or automated semantic validation. If this pair merely reasserted that fixed JSON is false or that checker CLEAN proved a bell, it would fail the axis instead.
