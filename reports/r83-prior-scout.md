# Round 83 prior-art scout — negation and scope

## Finding

The proposal is **not an exact repeat** of r28–r32, but its novelty and claimed repair need narrowing. R28–r30 already establish the important negation ladder against a fixed record: lexical support (“I, Mira, carried…”), unsupported proximity (“…was near Mira”), then explicit local negation (“…did not carry…”). R30 expressly limits conflict to the clause’s scope and requires editorial ID plus same-proposition/occasion alignment; it rejects an all-time “never” or canon-falsity inference. Those rounds do **not** test whether negation distributes over two coordinated objects versus taking scope over a joint phrase. R31–r32 concern source-ID ambiguity and malformed-row admission, not natural-language negation.

R34 is the closest calibration precedent in method: source prose changes while cited JSON remains fixed, and review distinguishes what the changed prose supports from what inherited records assert. But its “saw” → “heard that” contrast is evidentiary modality (direct sight versus reported hearing), not negation scope. R36 similarly tests an explicit date mismatch against inherited timeline/ledger wording; it does not test scope. These are adjacent prose-to-record near-misses, not the same semantic probe.

## Exact source/record and semantic limit

The proposed base is `.shokunin/lore/corpus/fixtures/r33-tarin-envelope/`. Its newly authored fictional excerpt is “I, Tarin, saw Sella place an unlabeled paper envelope on the Lantern Harbor registry desk.” The matching `entities.json` claim (`entities[4].claims[0]`) and `timeline.json` event (`timeline[2]`) say Tarin reports **seeing Sella place** the envelope. The source row labels `tarin-note` a fictional first-person note and says the excerpt is newly authored fiction. These are attribution/record statements, not authenticated source evidence.

The proposed “I did not see Sella with an envelope” is not the r30 sentence with a narrower negation. It changes the predicate from *see Sella place* to *see Sella with* an envelope as well as changing the scope structure. On its ordinary joint reading, it denies seeing the Sella-with-envelope configuration; it does not say Tarin failed to see Sella at all, nor that he failed to see an envelope separately. More importantly, it does not encode the proposed “envelope-alone absence” claim: the copied claim/event assert a reported placement observation, not a separate observation/absence field for the envelope. A reviewer could flag the event-level report as incompatible with the changed clause only under the relevant same-person/occasion and editorial identity alignments. The clause alone cannot justify a distinct repair about an envelope-alone absence assertion that the JSON does not make.

The change may illustrate the familiar logical distinction between `not see(Sella AND envelope-together)` and separate negative claims about each entity, but ordinary-language “with” can also invite contextual ambiguity. Without a precise target record that separately encodes those two absences, a non-author’s “repair” choice is not established as materially different. Do not claim calibration improvement merely because the rewording supports a narrower paraphrase. A controlled scope probe would need an exact source claim that asserts both separate non-sightings and a clear contrast clause/map, then demonstrate a different permissible editorial action; otherwise reject the proposed materiality claim as underdetermined or semantic hair-splitting.
