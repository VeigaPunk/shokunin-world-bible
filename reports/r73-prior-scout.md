## What the inherited accounts actually say

- In `corpus/fixtures/r72-vela-slate-claim/vela-slate-note-excerpt.md`, Vela says she could read “KEEP THE EAST GATE CLEAR” on a slate, wonders whether cramped letters mean its writer hurried, and cannot tell who wrote it. Its `vela-slate-clause-map.md` treats these as Vela’s attributed report, distinguishes reading from tentative inference and knowledge limit, and says the citation does not authenticate slate, words, placement, author, or chronology. The r72 entity claim (`entities.json`, `vela` claims) paraphrases those points and cites `vela-slate-note`.
- The inherited `jorin-note-excerpt.md` is identical to r43’s: Jorin says he saw Vela on the Lantern Harbor east steps during what he called Bell-7 and did not see who, if anyone, struck a bell. It says nothing about a slate or its inscription. Its map cautions that a shared Bell-7 label does not establish shared occasion or chronology. This is an attributed sighting account, not a statement that Jorin witnessed Vela reading.
- Thus the r73 proposal would add a separate Jorin report of different wording he says he read. On the declared premise alone, that would be a **discrepancy between two reported wordings**. Neither inherited account identifies the slate as the same physical object, fixes when either reading occurred, or says an inscription remained unchanged. They therefore do not establish that both reports describe one persistent inscription, and the wording difference is not a verified contradiction. Nor does Jorin’s existing sighting account corroborate the reading.

## Checker and evidentiary limits

`corpus/fixtures/check_bible.py` validates table structure and source-ID resolution; for entity claims it checks nonempty statement/source ID and source resolution. It does not read Markdown excerpts or clause maps, compare their wording to claims, determine whether two claims concern the same object/time, or adjudicate semantic contradiction. A clean fixture result can show structural validity only. Exact clause mapping and the editorial comparison note require human inspection; neither turns fictional report text into authenticated evidence or canon.

## Prior-scout judgment and falsifiers

The proposed difference has plausible narrative utility as two *attributed reports with different wording*, but that is not yet independently reviewed and is not a verified contradiction. The distinction from the r72 account is potentially meaningful if the new excerpt contributes an exact, source-attributed wording report while explicitly leaving inscription identity, time, and source unresolved. It is falsified or should be narrowed if the new line is redundant/decorative, if its prose/map implies Jorin saw Vela read or that both saw the same persistent slate, or if the comparison calls the difference proof of one inscription’s alteration, a liar, a canon fact, or a direct contradiction.

A non-author should compare literal excerpts, exact claim and clause pointers, and the comparison note. Require them to state separately: (1) each speaker’s reported words; (2) whether either text establishes same slate/inscription and same reading time; and (3) what remains unresolved about identity, time and source. The acceptable bounded conclusion, if supported by the eventual text, is “the reports give different wording; whether they concern the same inscription/occasion is open,” not “the inscription contradicts itself.” No depth admission follows from this prior-art scout alone. Coverage/calibration improvement is not established here. No inference about source truth, canon, rights, or game mechanics.
