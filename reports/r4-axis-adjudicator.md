# Round 4 — final per-axis adjudication

**Decision: do not admit round 4 as currently implemented.** The exact-string detector improves narrow coverage, but its hard failure harms calibration. Admission requires at least one improved axis **and none harmed**; the latter condition is not met. This is not a claim that legitimate homonyms are canon errors.

| Axis | Verdict | Evidence and boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Depth | **Unchanged** | The move adds a pairwise display-string comparison, not a richer canon model, source text, identity evidence, or disambiguation. Both entity IDs and records are retained. The invented `registry` source ID resolves, but its metadata does not attest the literal names. The claims say “Mira One” and “Mira Two,” while both duplicate-fixture `name` fields say `Mira`; no quoted in-world source text establishes those exact display names. Do not claim source verification or an actual contradiction. |
| Coverage | **Improved** | Baseline `5ceb0e4` had no duplicate-display-name check. The paired fixtures differ only at `entities[1].name`: `name-unique` exits 0; `name-duplicate` exits 1 with one finding identifying both locations and distinct IDs. The saved seven-case run in `corpus/live/runs/20261012T163450Z/`, non-author reviewer, and independent verifier agree. The verifier compared five old cases against archived baseline with identical status/stdout, checked 25/25 old JSON tables unchanged, probed duplicate IDs and a case variant, and checked 37 fixture files unchanged after execution. Coverage is only exact case-sensitive string equality among schema-valid, uniquely identified rows; it does not detect aliases, variants, or identity. |
| Calibration | **Harmed** | The LOG clarification and README properly say “editorial review,” “not proof,” and “do not merge”; the diagnostic itself says “review, do not merge.” Nevertheless the CLI emits `VIOLATION:` and exit **1** for every exact homonym, indistinguishable in status/category from a malformed reference or timeline contradiction. Neither schema nor source establishes a uniqueness invariant. A legitimate homonym therefore turns a canon-consistent bible into a failing one. Prose qualification reduces the risk of an attentive reader misinterpreting it, but does not prevent machine consumers or status-only users from treating a review candidate as a violation. The unverified exact-name attestation further forbids stronger provenance claims, although citation-ID resolution alone is a pre-existing limit rather than an additional proven regression. |

The reviewer found no defect **within the declared detector scope**, but explicitly raised the homonym/exit-status and name-attestation concerns. The verifier established replay correctness, not semantic validity of classifying a homonym as a violation. The critic recommended warning-only unless uniqueness is an explicit convention. Thus mechanical success does not settle the calibration axis. A separate warning/review status, or an explicit opt-in sourced uniqueness rule with appropriately attested names, would need its own evidence and adjudication; neither exists in this round. No code, fixture, LOG, or commit was changed by this adjudication.
