## Is an entity claim without an event semantically incomplete?

Not categorically. The checker’s entity schema requires `claims` to be a list; each claim is required to have a nonempty `statement` and `source_id`, and it checks that the source resolves (`check_bible.py`, `FIELDS['entities']` and the entity-claim loop). It does not require an associated timeline event or any claim-to-event link. Thus a single sourced Vela entity claim can represent the bounded proposition “Vela reports reading X and tentatively inferring Y,” provided the statement itself retains that attribution and distinction and the source is the fictional excerpt. An event row is not a prerequisite for the claim to be attributed or intelligible. Omitting one is therefore not automatically an arbitrary or invalid decoration choice—especially given Round 71’s documented calibration harm from forcing an unlocated report into shared tick order (`reports/r71-axis-adjudicator.md`).

But this schema fact is not proof that an entity-only slate claim has the same semantic function as an event representation. The checker’s timeline rows carry `entity_id`, `place_id`, `tick`, `depends_on`, `description`, and `source_id`; an entity claim has no structured actor/action/place/order fields or pointer to an event. The checker never reads source prose or clause maps, determines that a claim is an observation event, or verifies its meaning. If a later reader needs to find where/when Vela read the slate, compare that action with another event, or distinguish it structurally from a general Vela assertion, the claim alone cannot answer those questions unless its statement supplies bounded context—and adding such context still does not create structured timeline semantics. The lack of a timeline row is thus a deliberate loss of event-level organization, not evidence by itself that the proposition is meaningless.

The declaration’s case for depth is specifically an *observation/inference boundary*: Vela reports what she read and separately marks an uncertain inference about a maker. That distinction can reside in the claim sentence and cited excerpt; an event row would add structure but risks unsupported chronology. Yet it is only a candidate distinction, not established depth. A new slogan plus “maybe the writer hurried” may be arbitrary decorative content unless the literal report makes the partial-witness perspective materially legible beyond Vela’s inherited east-steps/bell/non-sighting account. Jorin reports seeing Vela at the east steps, not reading a slate (`r43-jorin-vela-sighting/jorin-note-excerpt.md` and `jorin-clause-map.md`); neither inherited account supplies a slate or maker inference. Novelty from absence in those accounts is not by itself narrative value. Round 71’s independent reviewer judged the actual close-reading / tentative-inference distinction a narrow depth gain, while noting the generic slogan could be decorative (`reports/r71-reviewer.md`, “Depth and calibration judgment”); that judgment concerned its concrete excerpt and does not transfer automatically to a changed entity-only candidate.

## Falsification checks and limits

For the delivered candidate, compare the exact source excerpt, sole Vela claim and clause map. The claim should say Vela *reports* reading the quoted wording, and separately attribute any uncertainty to her; it should not convert text into an established world fact or imply known authorship. Check that the in-world source is explicitly newly authored/unverified, that every material clause maps to the exact excerpt span and the same claim/source, and that no structured row silently supplies a place or time absent from the report. In particular, absence of a timeline event should not be described as evidence that an event did not happen or that the report has a verified position. Conversely, if the claim needs timeline-only context to make its central assertion intelligible, that is evidence the entity-only representation is semantically incomplete for the stated use.

A clean checker run can establish required fields and resolvable citations, not excerpt support, characterization, truth, plausibility, chronology, or canon. The inherited r43 Vela claim/event are separately attributed reports of hearing and non-sighting; they do not corroborate the proposed reading. A clause map is editorial review aid, not checker validation. The r71 report demonstrates the concrete tick-ordering risk, but does not prove every possible event encoding is unsafe.

**Provisional verdict:** entity-claim-only is structurally and semantically capable of representing an attributed statement, so the omission of an event is not inherently arbitrary or a defect. Whether it is *sufficient for this depth claim* remains unproved without the exact claim, excerpt and map. If these make the read-versus-inference distinction clear and materially useful as Vela’s reported perspective, a sole cited claim can preserve that distinction without inventing timeline position. If the words are merely decorative or depend on unstated event context, depth is not established. Do not infer slate truth/existence, writer identity, chronology, canon, rights, or game mechanics.
