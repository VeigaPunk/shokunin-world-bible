## Novelty challenge and falsifiable assessment

R41 is a serious novelty challenge, not merely background: it already compares a source’s stronger direct identification (“heard the registry bell strike twice”) with unchanged JSON’s weaker “she took to be” wording, and concludes that the fixed JSON is logically compatible but under-specific (`.shokunin/lore/reports/r41-scribe.md`, “Decision table” and “Precedent and novelty assessment”; `.shokunin/lore/corpus/fixtures/r41-vela-direct-bell/strength-contrast-map.md:1-3`). R34 also distinguishes direct seeing from “heard that” hearsay, where unchanged “reports seeing” JSON lacks direct-sighting support (`.shokunin/lore/reports/r34-scribe.md`, “Decision table”). Thus r46 is not novel merely because it changes modality/confidence or fixes JSON while changing prose.

The possible distinction is the **referent**: r41 concerns how confidently Vela identifies the sound as a bell; r46 concerns whether Jorin’s visually observed person is Vela at all. It reverses the strength direction (direct → tentative), and the disputed referent gates the cross-account location link. That is a concrete, manually falsifiable identification calibration, but it remains close to the existing r41 source-to-record strength mismatch. A non-author reviewer should reject novelty if this amounts only to the same generic stronger/weaker phrasing comparison; admit at most a narrow calibration gain if they demonstrate that the person-identity uncertainty changes whether Jorin’s excerpt supports an unqualified Vela-location record. No depth or coverage gain follows automatically.

**Risks:** (1) treating “someone I took to be Vela” as direct identification; (2) overcorrecting to “not Vela” or asserting an alternative identity; (3) calling the fixed JSON false/incompatible when it is logically possible; (4) treating `harbor`, same tick, or Bell-7 wording as proof of a shared person/occasion; (5) treating fictional source citation or checker cleanliness as truth/entailment; (6) claiming objective corroboration, canon, or a resolved Tarin/Sella ledger.

**Falsifiable go/no-go:** GO only if a non-author reviewer can (a) classify the proposed excerpt as Jorin reporting a tentative identification, not direct knowledge that the person is Vela; (b) classify the unchanged claim/event as logically compatible but more definite than the excerpt directly supports; (c) state the Vela-location connection conditionally, without shared-occasion or canon claims; and (d) explain why this person-referent distinction is materially different from r41’s already admitted confidence contrast. Any verdict that the person is objectively non-Vela, that the JSON is logically impossible, that the accounts share an occasion, or that the r46 novelty is only a restatement of r41 falsifies the proposed case for GO.
