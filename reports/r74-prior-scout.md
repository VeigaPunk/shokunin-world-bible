## Scout assessment and falsifier

**Potential novelty is narrow and conditional; not yet a depth admission.** A concise table could add editorial usefulness beyond r73 if each alternative has a distinct, specific *kind of in-world evidence to seek*, explains what outcome would discriminate it, cites the relevant r72/r73 pointers, and leaves the result unresolved. Merely expanding r73’s three open questions into hypothesis labels, or asking generally for “more evidence,” is duplication, not a material new decision aid. Evidence suggestions must be requests, not invented extant documents/facts (for example, do not assert that a second slate, retained copy, or authoritative record exists).

A non-author reviewer can falsify the move by comparing the eventual table with r73 `editorial-comparison.md`: if they cannot identify a different actionable evidence test per row, if the table chooses/privileges one account, or if its hypotheses imply surface continuity/change, then depth is unchanged or calibration is harmed. To pass narrowly, they should be able to say what new observation would discriminate each live possibility without claiming it exists or deciding the reports’ truth. The inherited structural checker is not a prose/map validator and cannot establish that editorial usefulness. No claims here about source truth, canon, real-world rights, or mechanics.
