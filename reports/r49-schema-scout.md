# Round 49 schema scout — carbon provenance representability

## Finding

The existing tables can structurally encode a carbon item and an ordered, source-ID-cited chain, but the proposed chain is not semantically safe as an unqualified holder history. The `provenance` contract's `to_holder` names the next holder after a transfer; the checker validates field shape, IDs, source uniqueness, order, and predecessor consistency—not whether an authored clause entails possession or receipt. On present evidence, the only warranted new fact would be that newly authored fictional Sella text *reports* that she handed the same carbon to a person she calls Jorin. It cannot authenticate a completed transfer or Jorin's receipt. Do not write `to_holder: jorin` as though it were established custody unless the record is explicitly qualified as reported assertion; there is no such qualification in the schema.

## Representability and caveats

- A unique item ID such as `carbon`, with first appearance/claim source and an optional provenance list, is mechanically representable. A source list may cite `registrar-copy` for a Sella-reported initial holding only if the field is clearly understood as that report, not proof. There is no explicit item identity key beyond the entity ID; “same carbon” is Sella's textual assertion, not physical authentication.
- A new source/excerpt can encode a first-person report of handing it to Jorin. However, the current provenance row `{from_holder: sella, to_holder: jorin}` reads as an actual chain transition. If the schema is retained unchanged, the chain would overstate the source unless the prose says receipt/possession and an authorial policy permits recording reported claims as assertions rather than facts. A Sella-authored note alone cannot confirm Jorin received/retained it. Safest schema-only choice: omit the handoff from `provenance`; represent only the attributed report in source text/timeline if that narrative is judged worthwhile. No checker change is warranted by this scout.
- Tick values are chain-local ordinal markers. A second chain entry must be numerically later than the initial one, but this is not evidence of when the transfer occurred or relation to timeline tick 3 / “later.” Timeline dependency may express that the reported note follows `registry-entry`; it does not date a handoff occurring in that note. Do not create a made-up acquisition time or connect clocks.
- The blurred oval remains unknown-origin. Neither an item called “carbon” nor adjacency to Blue Seal establishes mark identity, carbon/envelope/route-strip equivalence, destination, or holder's rights.

## Falsifiable gate and distinct depth assessment

**Go for a provenance chain only if** the authored clause and representation are explicitly claim-level/report-attributed, and a non-author confirms a reader cannot mistake `to_holder: jorin` for established receipt or actual custody. If the existing schema cannot convey that distinction, the chain is a no-go; omit it, or treat the proposal as an attributed narrative statement without provenance. Falsify support by removing explicit “the carbon I kept” anaphora or the handoff verb/name: then same-item link or handoff row is unsupported. Falsify receipt by asking whether Jorin independently acknowledges/receives it: absent that, no receipt assertion is admissible. A CLEAN checker run cannot satisfy this test.

**Depth is distinct but not established:** a reported custody action would differ narratively from r10 retention and r15 inspection, and from r6's generic mechanics; however it is a new Sella-authored assertion, not independent authentication. It improves depth only if a non-author judges the bounded attributed action itself materially connected and non-redundant, with no claim that rows or citations prove transfer. If only an unqualified provenance chain supplies the apparent novelty, reject. Coverage/calibration are unchanged absent checker semantics or an independently tested calibration delta.
