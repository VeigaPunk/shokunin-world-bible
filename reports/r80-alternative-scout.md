# Round 80 alternative scout — two-beat scene presentation

## Scope and prior bar

I read the Round 80 declaration in `.shokunin/lore/LOG.md` and the relevant r77/r79 reports, including r77 critic/story scout, r79 narrative/critic/simplifier, blind and adversarial readers, and the independent reviewer; I also checked the r73 clause/source/citation scout and reviewer. This is a report-only alternative, not a candidate fixture or an axis verdict.

R79 failed the materiality bar because the narrator gave a preference and its own rationale, and the editorial note merely repeated that rationale. A citation ID and a structurally valid claim would not make that authored rationale independent support. R73 is a different case: separately attributed accounts actually contain different reported wording, while their comparison stays text-level and leaves identity/occasion open. Round 80 asks for a deliberate action followed by a separately described observable response, so the response—not a second sentence explaining the actor’s preference—must discriminate between scene presentations.

## One bounded candidate and two genuinely different choices

Possible newly authored, explicitly fictional excerpt (not existing corpus text or evidence):

> I laid my map between us with my blue-marked crossing toward the door. Mara turned it around and tapped a different crossing.

This proposes a deliberate placement and a second character’s distinct, observable response. It supplies no motive, claim about who is right, named institution, date, or connection to the slate reports. The unnamed map and crossing are invented particulars, not facts about canon geography.

Two competing scene presentations:

1. **One-person plan:** keep the map oriented as the narrator placed it; frame the blue mark as the scene’s operative proposal, with Mara only present. This treats the first action as the meaningful beat.
2. **A negotiated disagreement:** show Mara physically turning the map and pointing to the other crossing; compose the scene around two competing marks / a change of orientation, not around the narrator’s plan alone.

The discriminator is the second, separately observable action: **Mara turns the map and taps a different crossing.** If that response is in the excerpt, it supports presentation 2 over a one-person-plan staging; without that response, the passage gives no basis to prefer the negotiated-disagreement staging. This is a visible change to the shared object and the point under discussion, not an explanation that repeats the narrator’s motive. It can change blocking and what the reader sees, rather than merely supply an adjective such as “careful.” The choice is narrow and scene-local; it does not establish a real disagreement or a durable trait.

## Materiality and circularity test

This is a **candidate for testing, not a claim that depth improved**. An independent reader should be able to name both alternatives and cite the map rotation/different crossing as the discriminator, without an editor’s gloss about trust, cooperation, danger, or who is correct. Reject it if the reader sees only decorative hand movement, if the second beat is paraphrased as the narrator’s reason, or if the scene decision is just “include the sentence because the sentence says so.” Also reject any claim that the authored response independently proves a world fact: both beats are created fiction. The added response makes a concrete alternative scene composition legible, but its existence is still a creative premise, not external corroboration or a reason it must be canon.
