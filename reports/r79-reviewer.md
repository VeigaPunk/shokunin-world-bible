## Scope and evidence

I reviewed the Round 79 declaration and actual candidate fixture: Iren’s excerpt, source and entity claim, clause map, and the fixture’s inherited r73 comparison; I also read the r79 blind/adversarial-reader reports, executor report, r77 reviewer and final adjudication, and r73 reviewer. ## Decision-use test and r73/r77 comparison

The excerpt says: “I wrote the names in the order people reached my desk, not the order the clerk called them. If I copied the clerk’s order, the people who had waited longest would look as though they came last.” The proposed action—display arrival order rather than clerk-call order—is concrete and observable. It is not a generic personality inference like the r77 latch note’s possible attentiveness/persistence cue. The adversarial reader is right that the literal contrast and stated consequence can survive removal of title, authority, and slate linkage.

But the candidate does **not demonstrate a material useful decision beyond its own invented rationale**. The excerpt supplies both the choice and the reason for that choice; the editorial note then repeats that reason as the warrant to depict the choice. The blind reader’s sealed result captures the limit: absent the invented passage, the fixture gives no basis to prefer either ordering, and the choice’s value is premise-dependent. This is an internally coherent authorial staging choice, but its citation and clause map add no independent constraint, alternative evidence, or editorial consequence. It does not show why this particular detail earns distinct space beyond faithfully staging the newly written sentence. That is more specific than generic texture, yet still self-justifying as evidence of a depth gain.

R73 is a useful contrast: its two separately attributed reports actually supply different quoted wording (“CLEAR” / “OPEN”), supporting a bounded comparison and unresolved editorial question without asserting a shared slate or occasion. R79’s arrival-order passage has no such independent comparison; its specific use is derived entirely from its own invented premise. Like r77, new fiction and a valid source pointer alone do not prove material usefulness. The adversarial report’s narrow fiction-local GO identifies a coherent possible choice, not independent evidence that the declared material-depth threshold has been met.

## Claim, source, and inference boundaries

The entity claim accurately labels its content as Iren’s report and paraphrases both clauses; the source row calls the note newly authored, fictional and unverified. The clause map preserves the exact text and correctly says the rationale is the narrator’s stated concern, not an independently verified consequence. I find no excerpt-to-claim overstatement that turns the conditional “would look” into a general rule.

“Desk,” “clerk,” “people reached,” and “waited longest” evoke a queue and could prompt readers to imagine an institution or procedure. The source note and clause map explicitly deny authentication and established procedure, while the editorial choice is confined to the hypothetical scene. Nothing names an institution, policy, actual event, title, or canon fact. Thus this is an inference risk to keep bounded, not an asserted institutional or identity claim and not demonstrated calibration harm. The candidate makes no link to the r72/r73 slate, its people, or any occasion; mere inheritance of those fixture rows does not establish one.

**Overall: NO-GO** for the declared material depth move. I agree the choice is fiction-local and concrete, but not that this candidate establishes useful depth beyond self-justifying invented prose. This is not a finding that the scene choice is invalid as creative writing.

**Falsifier / reversal condition:** reconsider depth if a non-author editor, given a bounded scene and meaningful competing presentation options, identifies a specific useful consequence of the arrival-order display that is not merely the passage repeating its own preference—for example, a concrete scene-reading or characterization consequence supported by text beyond the rationale itself—and that decision survives stripping institutional authority and unrelated slate links. A citation ID, cleaner paraphrase, or restatement of “longest-waiting people would look last” would not reverse this judgment. If the claim or editorial use instead treats this fictional queue as an established institution, procedure, actual event, or connected slate occasion, calibration should be reconsidered as harmed. No checker result could settle prose authenticity, rights, mechanics, source truth, or canon.
